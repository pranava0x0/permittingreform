         BIPARTISAN AMERICAN AFFORDABILITY AND JOBS ACT OF 2026

  Mr. LEE. Mr. President, I rise to discuss section 2107 of the 
Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, which will force data 
centers to pay for increased costs associated with artificial 
intelligence infrastructure so that Americans electricity bills do not 
increase.
  Section 2107 does this in two ways. First, subsection (a) requires 
the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission, or FERC, to require data 
centers to pay both the embedded and incremental costs associated with 
their interconnection to the grid. Second, subsection (b) creates a new 
section 229 of the Federal Power Act that establishes a Federal floor 
for grid costs that data centers must pay, while allowing States, if 
they choose, to charge additional costs to data centers, require them 
to provide their own generation, or credit excess revenues from data 
centers to other electricity customers in the State.
  These provisions are critical to ensuring that data centers cover all 
their own costs of interconnecting to the grid, including any necessary 
upgrades, and do not raise rates for American families, small 
businesses, or other electricity customers.
  Importantly, section 2107 does not change the existing jurisdictional 
boundaries between the Federal Government and the States. Under the 
Federal Power Act, FERC's authority is limited to rates for the 
transmission of electric energy in interstate commerce and the sale of 
electric energy at wholesale in interstate commerce, while States have 
exclusive jurisdiction over retail electric service, rate design, and 
resource planning.
  More specifically, section 2107(a) makes a change to FERC's 
transmission pricing policy statement and grants FERC no new authority 
over retail electric rates. Likewise, the new section 229(e) of the 
Federal Power Act expressly states that section 229 makes no 
modifications to existing Federal or State authority.
  As we work to meet historic demand for power, these provisions will 
support energy affordability, reduce costs for electricity ratepayers 
across the country, and protect American families from rising 
electricity costs.
