Data center and digital permitting bills compared

Compare BAAJA with data center siting and disclosure proposals, current E-NEPA law and the House and Senate ePermit bills.

Data center disclosure

What would communities learn about a data center’s power and water use?

BAAJA · Sec. 2114

BAAJA would have the Energy Information Administration revise Form 861 to collect electricity load attributable to data centers. p. 279, line 23 to p. 279, line 25

Data Center Transparency Act of 2026 (S. 5640)

For data centers consuming at least 50 megawatts each day, EPA would report at least quarterly on water use and emissions. EIA would publish state electricity totals and changes in household bills at least twice a year. Senate introduced text, §2 · September 30, 2026

Data Center Water and Energy Transparency Act of 2026 (S. 4213)

Operators of facilities with at least 25 megawatts of peak demand would report energy and water use to the state, or to federal agencies if the state has no program. Proposed facilities and expansions meeting the bill’s threshold would also report five-year projections. Senate introduced text, §3 · March 25, 2026

Data Center Community Empowerment Act of 2026 (S. 5645)

State, local and Tribal governments could request Energy Department help estimating power, water and infrastructure impacts. The department could not recommend whether to approve or reject an individual project. Senate introduced text, §2 · September 30, 2026

Data center siting

Who decides where a data center can be built, and when is environmental review required?

BAAJA · Sec. 1102

BAAJA would exclude a proposed agency action on a non-federal project from the definition of major federal action if the only federal involvement is a loan or loan guarantee. p. 17, line 1 to p. 17, line 3

Protecting Local Control of Data Centers Act (H.R. 10119)

For data centers on non-federal land, federal agencies could neither override state or local siting and permitting authority nor condition federal funding on its surrender. House introduced text, §2 · August 20, 2026

Public Lands Accountability Act (H.R. 10465)

A Bureau of Land Management authorization for a hyperscale data center on public lands would require an environmental impact statement. The bill would rule out categorical exclusions and require at least 45 days for written comments and a hearing in the affected community. House introduced text, §2 · September 16, 2026

Data center construction on federal land (S. 5658)

The bill would prohibit construction on National Forest System and Interior-managed lands, with specified Tribal lands excluded. Newly conveyed land would revert to the United States if used for a data center unless Congress specifically authorized that use. Senate introduced text, §1 · September 30, 2026

Digital permitting

Would agencies study a permitting portal or have to build one?

BAAJA · Sec. 1121

BAAJA would direct CEQ to implement the ePermit interagency system by December 1, 2028, to the maximum extent practicable. p. 92, line 18 to p. 92, line 21

E-NEPA (current law)

Congress directed the Council on Environmental Quality (CEQ) to study digital environmental review tools and report to Congress. A permit portal was a subject of that study; the statute did not direct CEQ to build it. 42 U.S.C. §4336d · NEPA §110

ePermit Act (H.R. 4503)

CEQ would publish data standards within 60 days and implementation guidance within 120 days. The target for the shared system is December 1, 2027, to the maximum extent practicable: one year earlier than BAAJA. Section 8 expressly conditions CEQ contracting authority on available appropriations. House-passed text, §§3, 5, 7 and 8 · December 9, 2025

ePermit Act (S. 3800)

The Senate text sets the same 60-day and 120-day deadlines and the same qualified December 1, 2027 system target as the House text. Its contracting clause omits the House version’s express appropriations condition. Senate introduced text, §§3, 5, 7 and 8 · February 5, 2026
