## Summary

Limits EPA's section 404(c) veto to the period between a complete application and permit issuance. Extends nationwide permits from 5 to 10 years, lets the Corps modify them mid-term, and requires it to keep nationwide permits 12, 14, 43, 57 and 58 for projects filling under 2 acres, which are deemed a minimal adverse effect. Reissuing a nationwide permit needs no Endangered Species Act consultation, only a programmatic environmental assessment under NEPA, and no National Historic Preservation Act consultation, so long as the permit does not loosen preconstruction requirements.

## Key points

- No section 404(c) veto before an application or after a permit issues ([p. 124, line 4 to p. 124, line 5](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=124))
- Nationwide permits last 10 years ([p. 125, line 2 to p. 125, line 3](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=125))
- Under 2 acres of fill is a minimal effect by statute ([p. 125, line 19 to p. 125, line 22](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=125))
- No ESA or NHPA consultation to reissue a nationwide permit that keeps its preconstruction requirements ([p. 128, line 7 to p. 128, line 10](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=128))

### Current law

EPA may veto a disposal site at any time, including after a permit issues (Mingo Logan v. EPA, D.C. Circuit, 2013). Nationwide permits run 5 years. A 2020 district court ruling held that reissuing Nationwide Permit 12 required Endangered Species Act consultation.

### EPRA 2024 (reported S. 4753)

Not in EPRA 2024.

### SPEED Act (House-passed H.R. 4776)
