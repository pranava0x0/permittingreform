## Summary

Congress finds it is not just and reasonable to charge data center, AI and cryptocurrency loads less than the embedded and incremental cost of the transmission they need. ([p. 226, line 25 to p. 227, line 4](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=226)) FERC must revise its 1994 transmission pricing policy within 270 days, propose a rule 270 days after that, and finish within a further year. ([p. 229, line 15 to p. 229, line 18](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=229))

New Federal Power Act section 228 covers data center loads of 20 megawatts or more. ([p. 232, line 9 to p. 232, line 12](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=232)) None of the incremental cost of serving them (generation, transmission, distribution) may be recovered from other customers. ([p. 234, line 23 to p. 235, line 1](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=234)) Recovery continues even if the data center leaves, and utilities must collect financial assurance before building upgrades. ([p. 235, line 23 to p. 236, line 1](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=235))

States may charge these loads more than cost and credit the surplus to other ratepayers. ([p. 236, line 14 to p. 236, line 17](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=236)) They may run open seasons that award service by ratepayer benefit, whatever the queue order. ([p. 237, line 1 to p. 237, line 4](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=237)) They may require the load to bring new generation, require curtailable service, and treat data centers less favorably than other industrial customers. ([p. 237, line 14 to p. 237, line 17](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=237)) The section applies to loads interconnecting after enactment. ([p. 240, line 1 to p. 240, line 3](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=240))

## Key points

- Data centers of 20 MW or more pay the full incremental cost of serving them ([p. 234, line 23 to p. 235, line 1](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=234))
- Costs follow the data center even after it exits ([p. 235, line 14 to p. 235, line 16](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=235))
- Up-front financial assurance before upgrades are built ([p. 235, line 23 to p. 236, line 1](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=235))
- States may auction grid access and require bring-your-own generation ([p. 236, line 23 to p. 237, line 1](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=236))
- Non-firm service gets a lower transmission price ([p. 230, line 9 to p. 230, line 14](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=230))
- Standardized study of cost-reduction options to avoid network upgrades ([p. 231, line 5 to p. 231, line 8](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=231))
- Incremental revenues credited to prevent double recovery from other ratepayers ([p. 230, line 1 to p. 230, line 5](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=230))
- Existing approved arrangements grandfathered ([p. 240, line 5 to p. 240, line 8](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=240))

### Current law

Cost allocation for large loads is set in individual state proceedings and FERC tariffs. FERC opened a large-load interconnection rulemaking in 2025. There is no federal floor.

### EPRA 2024 (reported S. 4753)

Not in EPRA 2024; data center cost allocation became a central issue in 2025-2026.

### SPEED Act (House-passed H.R. 4776)

Not in the SPEED Act.
