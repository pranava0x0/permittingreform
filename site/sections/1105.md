## Summary

Expands when an agency can skip an environmental document. ([p. 29, line 5 to p. 29, line 7](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=29)) An agency may apply one or a combination of categorical exclusions (but may not stack the same size-based exclusion repeatedly), may rely on a categorical exclusion Congress created, and may treat a state or tribal environmental review, or compliance with another federal law, as serving NEPA's function. ([p. 28, line 10 to p. 28, line 13](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=28)) A project sponsor can ask the lead agency to stop an environmental assessment and go straight to an environmental impact statement. ([p. 30, line 22 to p. 31, line 1](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=30)) If the lead agency decides no environmental document is needed, no other agency may prepare one. ([p. 31, line 8 to p. 31, line 9](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=31))

## Key points

- Combination of multiple categorical exclusions allowed ([p. 28, line 8 to p. 28, line 9](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=28))
- State or tribal review that serves a similar function can substitute for federal review ([p. 29, line 1 to p. 29, line 3](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=29))
- Sponsor can demand an EIS instead of an EA ([p. 30, line 22 to p. 31, line 1](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=30))
- Lead agency threshold determination binds all other agencies ([p. 31, line 8 to p. 31, line 9](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=31))

## Comparison notes

- [SPEED Act (H.R. 4776), engrossed in House](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr4776eh/html/BILLS-119hr4776eh.htm)
- [Energy Permitting Reform Act of 2024 (S. 4753), reported in Senate](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-118s4753rs/html/BILLS-118s4753rs.htm)

### Current law

NEPA section 106 (FRA 2023) already lets agencies apply their own or another agency's categorical exclusions; combining exclusions, statutory exclusions, state or tribal substitution and the binding threshold determination are new.

### EPRA 2024 (reported S. 4753)

EPRA 2024 did not amend NEPA section 106.

### SPEED Act (House-passed H.R. 4776)

The SPEED Act (sec. 2(b)) added the same two grounds for skipping a document (another statute or a state or tribal review that serves NEPA's function) and the same rule that a lead agency's threshold call binds other agencies. It also limited agencies to effects proximately caused by the project, which the Senate bill does not. Combining exclusions and the sponsor's right to request an EIS are Senate additions.
