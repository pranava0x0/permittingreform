## Summary

Gives applicants for permits that need no environmental document a right to sue in district court when the 1-year deadline in section 1402(c) is missed. ([p. 183, line 3 to p. 183, line 6](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=183)) The agency is excused if it proves the delay was caused by a disaster, a national emergency, fraud, withheld information, or a lapse in appropriations of more than 14 days. ([p. 183, line 15 to p. 183, line 17](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=183)) Courts decide within 120 days and can order a decision within 60 days. ([p. 184, line 22 to p. 184, line 24](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=184))

## Full statutory text

Extracted from the official PDF; line cites mark paragraph starts. Consult the PDF for exact layout.

[p. 182, line 9](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=182) (indent level 1)

(a) DEFINITIONS.—In this section:

[p. 182, line 10](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=182) (indent level 2)

(1) COVERED APPLICATION.—The term “covered application” means a complete application for 1 or more Federal authorizations or permits.

[p. 182, line 13](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=182) (indent level 2)

(2) FEDERAL AUTHORIZATION OR PERMIT.— The term “Federal authorization or permit” means an authorization, permit, record of decision, right-of-way, finding of no significant impact, verification, extension, construction and operations plan, biological opinion, incidental take statement, concurrence, or any other approval or order that is necessary, including any determination by a Federal department or agency that additional authorizations or permits are not necessary, for the construction or operation at full capacity of a project.

[p. 182, line 24](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=182) (indent level 1)

(b) RIGHT TO PETITION.—An applicant seeking a Federal authorization or permit for which an environmental impact statement or environmental assessment (as those terms are defined in section 3 of the National Environmental Policy Act of 1969) is not required may obtain judicial review of the compliance by a Federal agency with the deadline established under section 1402(c) with respect to the covered application by filing a complaint in a United States district court in which the applicable project is at least partially located.

[p. 183, line 9](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=183) (indent level 1)

(c) EXCEPTIONS.—The failure of a Federal agency to comply with the deadline established under section 1402(c) with respect to a covered application shall not be considered a failure to comply if the Federal agency proves by clear and convincing evidence that the failure was caused by—

[p. 183, line 15](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=183) (indent level 2)

(1) a natural disaster, act of God, or act of war that reasonably impaired the ability of the Federal agency to comply with the relevant deadline established under section 1402(c);

[p. 183, line 19](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=183) (indent level 2)

(2) a national emergency for which the only available response is to delay the decision on the Federal authorization or permit beyond the applicable deadline;

[p. 183, line 23](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=183) (indent level 2)

(3) the covered application being based on fraud or material misrepresentations of fact;

[p. 184, line 1](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=184) (indent level 2)

(4) the applicant withholding information necessary to the ability of the Federal agency to comply with the statutory obligations of the Federal agency in issuing a decision on the Federal authorization or permit after the Federal agency has made a reasonable and timely request for such information; or

[p. 184, line 7](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=184) (indent level 2)

(5) a lapse in appropriations of more than 14 days affecting 1 or more of the relevant Federal agencies with decisionmaking authority over the Federal authorization or permit.

[p. 184, line 11](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=184) (indent level 1)

(d) REMEDY.—If a court of competent jurisdiction finds that a Federal agency has failed to act in accordance with this section, the court shall—

[p. 184, line 14](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=184) (indent level 2)

(1) provide injunctive relief, as appropriate, to give effect to this section; and

[p. 184, line 16](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=184) (indent level 2)

(2) retain jurisdiction to ensure compliance with any such injunctive relief provided.

[p. 184, line 18](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=184) (indent level 1)

(e) EXPEDITED PROCEDURES; TIME TO DECIDE.— A reviewing court shall—

[p. 184, line 20](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=184) (indent level 2)

(1) establish an expedited schedule for briefing and disposition under this section; and

[p. 184, line 22](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=184) (indent level 2)

(2) issue a decision under this section as expeditiously as practicable, but not later than 120 days, after the date on which the petition is filed under subsection (b), absent extraordinary circumstances.

[p. 185, line 1](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=185) (indent level 1)

(f) ORDER.—A court awarding injunctive relief under subsection (d)(1) shall issue an order requiring a decision on the covered application not later than 60 days after the date on which the order is issued.
