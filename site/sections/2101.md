## Summary

Overhauls FERC's backstop siting authority under Federal Power Act section 216. ([p. 185, line 11 to p. 185, line 12](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=185)) FERC must issue a construction or modification permit for an interstate line of 230 kilovolts or more, or an advanced-conductor line at a voltage FERC sets, when it makes the required findings. ([p. 189, line 17 to p. 189, line 19](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=189)) The line no longer has to sit in a national interest corridor designated by the Energy Department. ([p. 199, line 11 to p. 199, line 15](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=199)) The state's clock for deciding now starts on the date the application is filed with the state. ([p. 189, line 2 to p. 189, line 5](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=189))

FERC must find the line is in the public interest, protects or benefits consumers, and improves reliability. ([p. 189, line 14 to p. 189, line 16](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=189)) For lines of 345 kilovolts or more the developer may file at FERC at the same time as at the state. ([p. 191, line 20 to p. 191, line 23](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=191)) FERC must take landowner input, consider the state record, and give a 45-day comment period. ([p. 188, line 11 to p. 188, line 12](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=188))

Costs go only to customers who benefit, in line with cost causation, and customers with no or trivial benefit cannot be charged involuntarily. ([p. 194, line 13 to p. 194, line 14](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=194)) Clean Water Act section 401 certifications and other state approvals under federal law become 'Federal authorizations' coordinated by FERC as lead agency (Interior for offshore), with challenges heard in the courts of appeals. ([p. 195, line 10 to p. 195, line 13](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=195)) ERCOT is excluded. ([p. 198, line 17 to p. 198, line 18](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=198))

## Key points

- No more national interest corridor prerequisite for FERC backstop siting ([p. 199, line 15 to p. 199, line 16](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=199))
- The state's clock starts on the date the application is filed with the state ([p. 189, line 2 to p. 189, line 5](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=189))
- 230 kV threshold; simultaneous state and FERC filing at 345 kV and above ([p. 189, line 17 to p. 189, line 19](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=189))
- Cost allocation limited to beneficiaries; no involuntary charges for trivial benefits ([p. 194, line 13 to p. 194, line 14](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=194))
- State 401 certifications for transmission folded into FERC-led federal authorization schedule ([p. 195, line 10 to p. 195, line 12](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=195))
- ERCOT excluded; public power and small co-ops only if they opt in ([p. 198, line 24 to p. 199, line 2](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=198))

## Comparison notes

- [SPEED Act (H.R. 4776), engrossed in House](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr4776eh/html/BILLS-119hr4776eh.htm)
- [Energy Permitting Reform Act of 2024 (S. 4753), reported in Senate](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-118s4753rs/html/BILLS-118s4753rs.htm)

### Current law

Section 216 (2005, amended 2021) says FERC 'may' site lines, and only inside National Interest Electric Transmission Corridors designated by the Energy Department. FERC can step in where a state lacks siting authority, has not decided within 1 year, has denied the application, or has set conditions that make the line uneconomic. Those grounds stay. The cost allocation and lead-agency changes are new.

### EPRA 2024 (reported S. 4753)

EPRA 2024 (sec. 401) made the same core change: FERC backstop siting without corridor designation, FERC action once a state has had 1 year, beneficiary-pays cost allocation and the trivial-benefit protection.

Differences: EPRA said FERC 'may' issue a permit after a 60-day comment period and set the floor at 100 kilovolts. This bill says 'shall' after 45 days and sets the floor at 230 kilovolts. It drops EPRA's findings that a line 'significantly reduce transmission congestion' and be 'consistent with sound national energy policy'. It adds simultaneous state and FERC filing at 345 kilovolts, the state record, an opt-in for public power and small co-ops, FERC-led coordination of state water certifications, and review of all related permits in the courts of appeals.

### SPEED Act (House-passed H.R. 4776)

Not in the SPEED Act, which amended only NEPA.
