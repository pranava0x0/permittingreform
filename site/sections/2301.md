## Summary

Rewrites section 106 of the National Historic Preservation Act. An 'adverse effect' must be reasonably foreseeable and must directly alter a historic property. ([p. 380, line 5 to p. 380, line 9](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=380)) Temporary alterations are excluded. ([p. 380, line 19 to p. 380, line 21](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=380)) Visual, atmospheric and audible effects are excluded unless they directly and significantly diminish the property. ([p. 380, line 13 to p. 380, line 17](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=380)) An 'undertaking' excludes projects whose only federal tie is a loan, guarantee or grant, the non-federal parts of a project, minor repairs, permit renewals and post-approval operations. ([p. 383, line 22 to p. 383, line 24](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=383))

The process gets deadlines: 30 days to decide whether a project is an undertaking, 60 days for the agency's report, 90 days for a determination, then 60 days of consultation, 45 days for Advisory Council comments and 30 days for a final decision. ([p. 386, line 8 to p. 386, line 11](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=386)) Consultation must be finished by the time the NEPA document is completed, unless the Secretary grants an extension for extraordinary circumstances. ([p. 398, line 3 to p. 398, line 6](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=398)) State and tribal preservation officers who do not respond in 30 days are passed over, and the agency may decide in its sole discretion that other considerations outweigh preservation. ([p. 400, line 19 to p. 400, line 21](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=400))

Courts give substantial deference and may only remand without vacatur or injunction; a preliminary injunction is available only for a likely violation of another law. ([p. 406, line 25 to p. 407, line 2](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=406)) Claims go to the courts of appeals, within 150 days, from parties who commented and suffered direct harm. ([p. 411, line 20 to p. 411, line 23](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=411)) A suit over a missed consultation deadline goes to a court where the project is located. ([p. 399, line 12 to p. 399, line 16](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=399)) Non-signatories cannot challenge programmatic agreements. ([p. 412, line 24 to p. 413, line 2](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=412)) State and tribal programs are evaluated every 2 years and lose grants if they miss consultation timelines. ([p. 373, line 18 to p. 373, line 22](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=373))

## Key points

- Adverse effect limited to direct physical alteration; visual and noise effects mostly excluded ([p. 380, line 5 to p. 380, line 7](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=380))
- Loans and grants alone no longer trigger section 106 ([p. 383, line 22 to p. 383, line 24](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=383))
- End-to-end deadlines; silence from a preservation officer after 30 days lets the agency proceed ([p. 400, line 19 to p. 400, line 21](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=400))
- Agency may find other considerations outweigh preservation 'in the sole discretion' of the official ([p. 391, line 13 to p. 391, line 17](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=391))
- 150-day limit, court of appeals jurisdiction, remand without vacatur ([p. 408, line 23 to p. 408, line 25](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=408))
- Later National Register listings do not reopen a review ([p. 413, line 14 to p. 413, line 17](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=413))

### Current law

Section 106 is one sentence requiring agencies to 'take into account' effects on historic properties; the process lives in Advisory Council regulations (36 CFR part 800) with no statutory deadlines, a broad definition of adverse effect that includes visual and audible intrusions, and ordinary APA review.

### EPRA 2024 (reported S. 4753)

Not in EPRA 2024. This is the first major permitting bill to rewrite section 106.

### SPEED Act (House-passed H.R. 4776)

Not in the SPEED Act.
