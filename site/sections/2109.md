## Summary

Requires FERC to make public utilities use advanced transmission technologies, including best-available conductors, dynamic line rating, power flow control and topology optimization, in new and upgraded lines, with a presumption that the cost is prudent. ([p. 261, line 13 to p. 261, line 16](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=261)) FERC issues a rule within 1 year requiring recurring benefit-cost evaluations; when benefits exceed costs the utility must deploy on FERC's timeline or face a penalty such as a lower return on equity. ([p. 262, line 8 to p. 262, line 11](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=262))

## Key points

- Mandatory use of grid-enhancing technologies when benefits exceed costs ([p. 263, line 16 to p. 263, line 19](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=263))
- Presumption of prudence for their cost ([p. 261, line 23 to p. 261, line 24](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=261))
- Return-on-equity penalty for not deploying ([p. 264, line 19 to p. 264, line 21](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=264))

### Current law

FERC Order No. 1920 requires regions to consider four such technologies; nothing compels deployment.

### EPRA 2024 (reported S. 4753)

EPRA 2024 allowed advanced conductors in backstop projects but had no deployment mandate.

### SPEED Act (House-passed H.R. 4776)

Not in the SPEED Act.
