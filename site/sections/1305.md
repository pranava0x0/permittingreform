## Summary

Lets any person seek review of a biological opinion in the D.C. Circuit within 150 days after it is made public. ([p. 154, line 17 to p. 154, line 20](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=154))

## Key points

- 150-day window and D.C. Circuit venue for biological opinion challenges ([p. 154, line 17 to p. 154, line 19](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=154))

## What others say it would do

Outside readings, quoted from their sources. These are claims about effects, not findings of this tracker.

- **Center for Biological Diversity and 214 other groups** (Environmental groups; opposes): Every Section 7 challenge would go straight to the D.C. Circuit, which has 11 active judges, and would have to be filed within 150 days.
  > The legislation would direct every Section 7 challenge to the D.C. Circuit
  [Coalition letter (Center for Biological Diversity), 2026-10-06](https://biologicaldiversity.org/programs/government-affairs/pdfs/Oppose-ESA-attacks-in-permitting-deal.pdf). On the bill text at [p. 154, line 16 to p. 154, line 18](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=154).
- **Earthjustice** (Environmental groups; concerns): Federal consultations would be challenged in the D.C. Circuit and state-run ones in district court, splitting review of the same law.
  > By contrast, challenges to consultations by states would be brought in district court, creating a balkanized system for judicial review
  [Earthjustice, 2026-10-20](https://earthjustice.org/feature/bipartisan-american-affordability-and-jobs-act-2026-impact-guide). On the bill text at [p. 147, line 6 to p. 147, line 8](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=147).
