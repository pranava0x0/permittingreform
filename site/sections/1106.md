## Summary

Rewrites NEPA section 107, the core of the review process. Environmental assessments are due in 1 year and environmental impact statements in 2 years, measured from the earlier of the agency's determination or its notice that the application is complete. A record of decision follows a final EIS within 45 days. Agencies in the review must decide all authorizations within 90 days, and agencies outside it within 120 days.

Lead agency designation gets a clock, with the CEQ Chairman resolving disputes within 21 days by a final, unreviewable decision. The Chairman can also order a resolution of interagency disputes, and that order counts as full compliance with any interagency consultation obligation under federal law.

The lead agency must drop any alternative that does not meet the purpose and need or the goals of a non-federal sponsor. Agencies need not consider science published after the notice of intent. Public scoping comment periods run 45 to 60 days, or 75 with the sponsor's consent.

If an agency defies a Chairman's dispute order and a deadline is missed, then after 30 days its permitting officers and senior executives must spend at least half their workweek on the overdue review. Once any deadline is 60 days overdue, the agency head may not travel more than 25 miles from the duty station until the document or permit issues. The Secretary of Defense is exempt. Project sponsors can sue over missed deadlines. The court must rule within 120 days and can order a decision within 60 days.

## Comparison notes

- [SPEED Act (H.R. 4776), engrossed in House](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr4776eh/html/BILLS-119hr4776eh.htm)
- [Energy Permitting Reform Act of 2024 (S. 4753), reported in Senate](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-118s4753rs/html/BILLS-118s4753rs.htm)

### Current law

The Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2023 set the 2-year EIS and 1-year EA deadlines, page limits of 150 pages for an EIS and 75 for an EA (both kept), and a sponsor's right to petition a court, which can order the agency to act within 90 days.

New here: the clock no longer starts at the notice of intent. A record of decision is due 45 days after the EIS and all permits within 90 or 120 days. Agencies need not consider science published after the notice of intent. The CEQ Chairman settles lead-agency and interagency disputes with final, unreviewable orders, and lead-agency disputes take 21 days, down from 40. Alternatives that miss the sponsor's goals are dropped. Missed deadlines can trigger the staff-time and travel sanctions, and courts must rule on deadline suits within 120 days.

### EPRA 2024 (reported S. 4753)

EPRA 2024 did not rewrite NEPA section 107; its deadlines were project-specific (energy leasing, rights-of-way).

### SPEED Act (House-passed H.R. 4776)

The SPEED Act (sec. 2(c)) kept the 2023 deadlines and added a science cutoff at the earlier of the application or the notice of intent, a requirement that the purpose and need 'meet the goals of the applicant', 21-day clocks for cooperating agencies, and a final agency action within 30 days of the finished document. It had no CEQ dispute orders and no sanctions on agency staff or agency heads.
