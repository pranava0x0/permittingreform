## Summary

A 150-day statute of limitations for any lawsuit challenging a federal agency's grant or denial of an authorization for an energy, carbon dioxide, mineral or wildfire fuel-reduction project, unless the underlying law sets a shorter one. ([p. 284, line 13 to p. 284, line 15](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=284)) Covered authorizations include leases, drilling permits, rights-of-way, mining plans, Endangered Species Act statements and permits, and forest restoration approvals. ([p. 281, line 13 to p. 281, line 16](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=281)) Agencies must publish the decision within 3 days. ([p. 284, line 18 to p. 284, line 21](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=284)) Courts must expedite the case, and a remand cannot give the agency more than 180 days unless the law requires longer. ([p. 284, line 22 to p. 284, line 25](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=284)) Agencies must tell the project sponsor about any suit within 30 days. ([p. 286, line 1 to p. 286, line 3](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=286))

## Key points

- 150 days to sue over energy and mineral project decisions under any federal law ([p. 284, line 12 to p. 284, line 14](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=284))
- 180-day cap on agency action after a court remand ([p. 285, line 8 to p. 285, line 10](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=285))
- Covers oil and gas, mining, offshore, geothermal, rights-of-way and forest fuel-reduction projects ([p. 281, line 13 to p. 281, line 16](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=281))

### Current law

The default limit for suits against the United States is 6 years; some statutes set shorter periods (60 days for Natural Gas Act orders, 2 years for FAST-41 covered projects).

### EPRA 2024 (reported S. 4753)

Carried over from EPRA 2024 (sec. 101) nearly word for word: the 150-day limit, the 180-day remand cap and the 30-day notice to sponsors. This version adds leases, rights-of-way and easements to the definition and starts the clock when the decision is made public, which must happen within 3 days.

### SPEED Act (House-passed H.R. 4776)

The SPEED Act's 150-day limit applied to NEPA claims; this section reaches claims under any federal law for energy and mineral projects.
