## Summary

Lets an Indian tribe grant rights-of-way across its own tribal land without Interior Department approval, if the grant follows a tribal regulation that Interior has approved. ([p. 291, line 11 to p. 291, line 13](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=291)) The tribal regulation must include an environmental review with public comment. ([p. 292, line 21 to p. 292, line 24](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=292)) Interior has 180 days to approve or disapprove, and its approval is not subject to NEPA, the National Historic Preservation Act or the Endangered Species Act. ([p. 293, line 22 to p. 294, line 1](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=293))

## Key points

- Tribes can approve their own rights-of-way under an approved tribal regulation ([p. 291, line 8 to p. 291, line 10](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=291))
- Interior has 180 days to approve or disapprove a tribe's regulation ([p. 293, line 22 to p. 294, line 8](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=293))

### Current law

Rights-of-way across tribal land need Interior approval under the 1948 Act and 25 CFR part 169. The HEARTH Act of 2012 already lets tribes approve their own surface leases under approved tribal regulations.

### EPRA 2024 (reported S. 4753)

Carried over from EPRA 2024 (sec. 205).

### SPEED Act (House-passed H.R. 4776)

Not in the SPEED Act.
