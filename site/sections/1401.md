## Summary

For projects with non-federal sponsors, a federal agency may not revoke, suspend, amend, or otherwise interfere with a federal authorization in effect on or after September 16, 2026. ([p. 159, line 12 to p. 159, line 15](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=159))

There are five exceptions. ([p. 159, line 20 to p. 159, line 21](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=159)) A court can order the action. ([p. 159, line 20 to p. 159, line 21](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=159)) The holder can have materially breached the permit or the law, or obtained the permit by fraud. ([p. 159, line 23 to p. 160, line 2](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=159)) The action can be needed to prevent specific, urgent, substantial and proximate harm to life, property, national security or defense, based on new information not considered in the original review. ([p. 160, line 6 to p. 160, line 9](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=160)) Or the holder can ask for it. ([p. 160, line 13 to p. 160, line 16](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=160))

Agencies must give written notice with the statutory authority and evidence. ([p. 160, line 24 to p. 161, line 2](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=160)) The sponsor can sue in district court, where the agency carries the burden by clear and convincing evidence and classified information is reviewed in camera. ([p. 162, line 4 to p. 162, line 7](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=162)) A winning sponsor gets the permit reinstated, an injunction against repeat interference without a court order, attorney fees, delay costs, and an additional 25 to 50 percent of all project costs incurred, paid from the Judgment Fund without appropriation. ([p. 163, line 3 to p. 163, line 7](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=163))

Where a stop-work order issued within the year before enactment was enjoined, the same judge keeps jurisdiction. ([p. 165, line 13 to p. 165, line 17](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=165))

## Key points

- No permit revocation absent a court order, breach, fraud, new-information emergency, or the holder's request ([p. 159, line 20 to p. 159, line 21](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=159))
- Burden on the government by clear and convincing evidence ([p. 162, line 4 to p. 162, line 6](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=162))
- Damages of 25 to 50 percent of costs incurred, plus fees and delay costs, from the Judgment Fund ([p. 164, line 3 to p. 164, line 6](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=164))
- Where a stop-work order from the year before enactment was enjoined, the same judge hears any new attempt ([p. 165, line 1 to p. 165, line 3](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=165))
- Cutoff date of September 16, 2026 for permits in effect ([p. 159, line 14 to p. 159, line 15](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=159))

## Comparison notes

- [SPEED Act (H.R. 4776), engrossed in House](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr4776eh/html/BILLS-119hr4776eh.htm)
- [Energy Permitting Reform Act of 2024 (S. 4753), reported in Senate](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-118s4753rs/html/BILLS-118s4753rs.htm)

### Current law

New. Agencies today can suspend or revoke leases and permits under each statute and the permit's own terms, subject to APA review with the burden on the challenger.

### EPRA 2024 (reported S. 4753)

Not in EPRA 2024; this title responds to 2025 and 2026 events.

### SPEED Act (House-passed H.R. 4776)

The SPEED Act (sec. 2(b)) had a shorter version of this rule inside NEPA: the same five exceptions, a clear-and-convincing standard 'as appropriate and where feasible', and APA review in the court of appeals. It had no damages and no cutoff date, and its section 4 exempted any agency action the administration had moved to reconsider since January 20, 2025, which covered the offshore wind approvals then under review. The Senate bill has no such exemption, covers permits in effect on or after September 16, 2026, shifts the burden of proof to the agency, adds money damages, and keeps the same judge on projects whose stop-work orders were already enjoined.

(a) DEFINITION OF FEDERAL AUTHORIZATION OR PERMIT.—The term “Federal authorization or permit” means an authorization, permit, record of decision, right-of-way, finding of no significant impact, verification, extension, construction and operations plan, biological opinion, incidental take statement, concurrence, or any other approval or order that is necessary, including any determination by a Federal department or agency that additional authorizations or permits are not necessary, for the construction or operation at full capacity of a project.

(1) IN GENERAL.—For projects or actions with non-Federal sponsors or holders of a Federal authorization or permit, a Federal department or agency shall not take any new action that would revoke, rescind, withdraw, terminate, suspend, amend, or alter a Federal authorization or permit in effect on or after September 16, 2026, or take any other action to interfere with or prevent the construction or operation at full capacity of a project that has secured all necessary Federal authorizations and permits, unless—

[p. 159, line 20](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=159) (indent level 3)
