## Summary

Lets a hardrock mine operator locate as many 5-acre mill sites on public land as are reasonably necessary for waste rock, tailings and other operations under an approved plan of operations, for a $400 annual fee per site. Mill sites convey no mineral rights and cannot be patented. Hardrock minerals on acquired federal lands become leasable. Mill site fees and those lease receipts go to a new Abandoned Hardrock Mine Fund for cleanup.

## Key points

- A mine operator may locate as many 5-acre mill sites as its plan of operations needs ([p. 308, line 1 to p. 308, line 15](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=308))
- $400 per mill site per year, paid into an abandoned mine cleanup fund ([p. 311, line 12 to p. 311, line 13](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=311))

### Current law

Mill sites are capped at 5 acres each on non-mineral land. The Ninth Circuit held in 2022 (the Rosemont case) that waste rock cannot be stored on claims that lack a valuable mineral deposit.

### EPRA 2024 (reported S. 4753)

Carried over from EPRA 2024 (sec. 210, hardrock mining mill sites), including the cleanup fund.

### SPEED Act (House-passed H.R. 4776)

Not in the SPEED Act; the House passed a separate Mining Regulatory Clarity Act.
