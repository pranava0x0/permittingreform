## Summary

Sets an application clock: within 60 days an agency must say whether an application is complete, and within another 60 days it must tell the sponsor whether the action is excluded from NEPA, needs an environmental assessment, or gets a notice of intent for an EIS. Applicants must submit a list of all known permits, a record of stakeholder engagement, and a statement of purpose and need.

## Key points

- 60 days to declare an application complete; 60 more to pick the level of review ([p. 65, line 14 to p. 65, line 16](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=65))
- Applicants file a record of stakeholder engagement with the application ([p. 67, line 1 to p. 67, line 2](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=67))

### Current law

New. The current section 110 ordered CEQ to study a permitting portal; that study is replaced by the eNEPA mandate in section 1121.

### EPRA 2024 (reported S. 4753)

Not in EPRA 2024.

### SPEED Act (House-passed H.R. 4776)

The SPEED Act (sec. 2(c)(4)) had the same 60-day completeness check and 60-day level-of-review decision. The required list of permits, record of stakeholder engagement and purpose-and-need description are Senate additions.
