## Summary

Interior or the Forest Service must tell an eligible-project applicant within 30 days whether its right-of-way application is complete. If an EIS is needed, the agency has 90 days after finding the application complete to issue a notice of intent. Within 30 days after a complete application is submitted, it must issue any required cost-recovery agreement and grant, deny or defer the application. Deferral requires notice of remaining steps and agency timelines.

Within 180 days, both departments must develop or adopt categorical exclusions for listed low-disturbance renewable activities, with extraordinary-circumstances exceptions. The list includes previously analyzed disturbances under 5 acres, certain repeat activities, small solar projects, surveys and meteorological towers. BLM must issue standard renewable review standards within 2 years.

## Key points

- 30-day completeness and decision clocks for renewable rights-of-way on public land ([p. 299, line 11 to p. 299, line 14](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=299))
- Categorical exclusions for small solar, met towers and geotechnical work within 180 days ([p. 301, line 22 to p. 301, line 24](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=301))
- Storage added to the definition of an eligible renewable project ([p. 303, line 22 to p. 303, line 22](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=303))

### Current law

No statutory deadlines for renewable right-of-way applications; Interior's July 2025 policy sent wind and solar decisions to the Secretary's office for sign-off.

### EPRA 2024 (reported S. 4753)

Carried over from EPRA 2024 (secs. 206 and 207): the right-of-way clocks, the low-disturbance exclusions, storage as an eligible project, and the BLM review standards. EPRA's national goal of at least 50 gigawatts of renewable energy authorized on federal land by 2030 is not in this bill.

### SPEED Act (House-passed H.R. 4776)

Not in the SPEED Act.
