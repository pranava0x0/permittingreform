## Summary

Rewrites Clean Water Act section 401 state certification. ([p. 103, line 12 to p. 103, line 13](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=103)) Certification is limited to water quality requirements. ([p. 104, line 19 to p. 104, line 22](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=104)) A state may deny only on clear and convincing evidence that no condition could make the project comply, and may impose only conditions that are necessary, least burdensome, and consistent with the federal permit. ([p. 105, line 3 to p. 105, line 7](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=105))

The process gets a prefiling meeting, a defined complete application, a 3-day receipt acknowledgment, a 90-day completeness notice, and a review period of up to 1 year that can be extended only if the applicant asks in writing and the certifying authority agrees in writing. ([p. 114, line 20 to p. 114, line 24](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=114)) Silence is a waiver. ([p. 115, line 10 to p. 115, line 11](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=115)) Denials must name the specific water quality requirement violated. ([p. 114, line 1 to p. 114, line 4](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=114)) On judicial review the burden of proof falls on the state by clear and convincing evidence. ([p. 117, line 5 to p. 117, line 7](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=117))

For interstate gas pipelines and bulk-power transmission lines, certification covers only direct point source discharges and must be decided within 9 months. ([p. 121, line 15 to p. 121, line 16](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=121))

## Key points

- Certification scope narrowed to water quality requirements; clear-and-convincing standard for denials ([p. 104, line 19 to p. 104, line 22](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=104))
- One-year cap, extendable only at the applicant's written request and with the certifying authority's written agreement ([p. 115, line 19 to p. 115, line 23](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=115))
- Burden of proof on the state in court ([p. 117, line 5 to p. 117, line 7](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=117))
- Interstate pipelines and transmission lines: point-source discharges only, 9-month clock ([p. 121, line 15 to p. 121, line 16](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=121))

### Current law

Section 401 lets states certify that a discharge complies with state water quality standards and 'any other appropriate requirement of State law', with a reasonable period not to exceed 1 year; EPA's 2020 and 2023 rules fought over the scope. The bill codifies a narrow scope similar to the 2020 rule, adds the clear-and-convincing standard and the point-source-only rule for pipelines and transmission.

### EPRA 2024 (reported S. 4753)

Not in EPRA 2024; Senate Republicans had sought section 401 limits in earlier bills such as Capito's 2023 RESTART Act.

### SPEED Act (House-passed H.R. 4776)

Not in the SPEED Act.
