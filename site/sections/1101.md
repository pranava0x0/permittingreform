## Summary

Rewrites NEPA's opening section. ([p. 4, line 9 to p. 4, line 10](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=4)) Adds a statement that NEPA prescribes a procedure for considering and informing the public about the reasonably foreseeable environmental effects of major federal actions, producing an informational document, and a savings clause that NEPA mandates no particular outcome. ([p. 5, line 11 to p. 5, line 13](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=5))

## Key points

- Codifies NEPA as a procedural statute whose product is an informational document ([p. 5, line 14 to p. 5, line 16](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=5))
- New savings provision: nothing in NEPA mandates a particular outcome ([p. 5, line 17 to p. 5, line 19](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=5))

## Selected statutory quotes

> Nothing in this Act mandates a particular outcome as a result of the use of the procedure described in subsection (b).

[p. 5, line 17 to p. 5, line 19](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=5)

States in the statute that NEPA is procedural, as the Supreme Court held in Seven County.

### Current law

Current NEPA section 2 states purposes only; the procedure and savings language is new.

### EPRA 2024 (reported S. 4753)

EPRA 2024 did not amend NEPA's purposes section.

### SPEED Act (House-passed H.R. 4776)

The SPEED Act (sec. 2(a)) added a subsection to NEPA section 2 calling the law 'a purely procedural statute' that 'does not mandate particular results'. The Senate text says the same thing in different words.
