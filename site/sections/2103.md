## Summary

Adds Federal Power Act sections 224 and 225. ([p. 205, line 12 to p. 205, line 13](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=205)) The Energy Department studies transmission constraints and congestion every 3 years and may identify constrained geographic areas. ([p. 205, line 15 to p. 205, line 17](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=205)) FERC must issue a rule within 180 days requiring every planning region to file a regional planning process and joint interregional processes with its neighbors within 2 years, updated every 4 years. ([p. 211, line 11 to p. 211, line 13](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=211)) The processes must use common assumptions, a fixed list of six benefits, and selection criteria of improved reliability, consumer benefit and public interest. ([p. 213, line 1 to p. 213, line 2](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=213))

Costs are allocated to beneficiaries roughly commensurate with benefits. ([p. 216, line 12 to p. 216, line 15](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=216)) Compliance with the FERC planning orders published on June 11, 2024, December 6, 2024 and April 28, 2025 (Order 1920 and its two rehearing orders) counts as compliance for regional planning. ([p. 217, line 3 to p. 217, line 6](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=217)) A project selected in a regional or interregional plan is deemed to meet most of the findings for a FERC backstop permit. ([p. 217, line 9 to p. 217, line 12](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=217)) Approving a planning process is not a major federal action under NEPA. ([p. 218, line 5 to p. 218, line 8](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=218))

## Key points

- Requires interregional transmission planning by statute ([p. 211, line 19 to p. 212, line 8](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=211))
- FERC rule in 180 days; regional filings in 2 years ([p. 211, line 11 to p. 211, line 13](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=211))
- Compliance with FERC's 2024 and 2025 planning orders (Order 1920) counts for the regional piece ([p. 217, line 1 to p. 217, line 4](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=217))
- Plan-selected projects get a fast path to a FERC siting permit ([p. 217, line 12 to p. 217, line 14](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=217))
- Six enumerated benefits; reliability first ([p. 213, line 19 to p. 213, line 21](https://pranava0x0.github.io/permittingreform/bill.pdf#page=213))

## Comparison notes

- [SPEED Act (H.R. 4776), engrossed in House](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr4776eh/html/BILLS-119hr4776eh.htm)
- [Energy Permitting Reform Act of 2024 (S. 4753), reported in Senate](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-118s4753rs/html/BILLS-118s4753rs.htm)

### Current law

FERC Orders 1000 and 1920 require regional planning by rule; interregional coordination is weak and there is no statutory requirement. DOE's triennial congestion study exists under section 216(a).

### EPRA 2024 (reported S. 4753)

EPRA 2024 (sec. 402) created the same sections 224 and 225 but required only interregional plans. This bill also requires regional planning processes, deems FERC Order No. 1920 compliance sufficient for the regional piece, and exempts public power and small co-ops unless they opt in. It drops EPRA's 60-day FERC dispute resolution between regions and FERC's power to impose a plan when regions fail to file one; the only consequence left is a 6-month extension.

### SPEED Act (House-passed H.R. 4776)

Not in the SPEED Act.
