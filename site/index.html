Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026

This bipartisan Senate bill would change federal permitting for energy and infrastructure projects and set what data centers pay for transmission service. Senate EPW Committee

2026-11-19: The Senate passed it, 62 to 33, without the managers' amendment. Roll call 270

2026-11-30: The House returns; no vote set yet. House schedule

Status

  • The bill goes back to the House, which is on a district work period through November 29. Congressional Record, House, November 19
  • On November 18 the Senate adopted the sponsors' substitute, SA 6883, 69 to 27, after voting 70 to 26 to waive Sen. Jeff Merkley's budget point of order against it. It then voted 65 to 31 to end debate on the bill. Senate roll call vote 268

It would add landowner protections for transmission permits, make federal permits public and raise the Historic Preservation Fund to $300 million a year. Senators had filed 172 amendments to the substitute or the bill by then. Congressional Record, November 17

Provisions

  • Review deadlines. Sets 2-year EIS and 1-year assessment deadlines, followed by deadlines for agency decisions. Extensions and enforcement conditions apply; the travel sanction limits official trips beyond 25 miles after a qualifying deadline is 60 days overdue.
  • Court challenges. Generally allows 150 days for NEPA challenges in a court of appeals, with direct-harm and conditional comment requirements. Limits remedies to remand, with a fix due within 180 days; preliminary injunctions remain available under specified conditions involving other law.
  • Issued permits. Restricts interference with permits in effect on or after September 16, 2026, subject to listed exceptions including the holder’s request. If the agency fails to meet its burden in court, the sponsor is entitled to specified relief, including a court-determined 25 to 50 percent of incurred project costs not otherwise recovered.
  • Data center costs. Newly connected data-center loads of 20 megawatts or more must cover their incremental electricity costs. Previously approved service arrangements remain in effect. The bill recognizes state authority to require new generation.
  • Transmission siting. FERC can permit interstate lines of 230 kilovolts and up that states have not approved, after making required findings, and planning regions must plan lines with their neighbors.
  • Actions outside NEPA. Excludes specified loan-only actions, qualifying work in existing grid and pipeline corridors, and listed oil, gas and geothermal activities from NEPA. Each exclusion has its own scope and conditions.
  • Clean Water Act. Narrows state water-quality certification review and generally limits it to one year; extensions require written agreement. Extends nationwide permits to 10 years.
  • Endangered Species Act. Cuts the standard formal consultation period from 90 days to 60, with extension rules. Lets approved state programs assume specified consultation responsibilities; applicants can opt for federal consultation.
  • Historic preservation and tribal consultation. Narrows the effects covered by historic-preservation review and sets deadlines. Adds a 150-day challenge window and limits remedies, with separate rules for missed deadlines.
  • Digital permitting. Requires a public tracker of defined transparency projects, and targets December 1, 2028 for an interagency ePermit system, to the maximum extent practicable.
