Amendments to the permitting bill

What the November 7 substitute changes in each section of the September 30 text, with page and line cites in both PDFs, other amendments filed or announced, and who has said they support or oppose them.

Key changes

  • Data center costs, Sec. 2107: Drops the September definition of covered load, which set a floor of 20 megawatts. Sept. 30 p. 232, line 9
  • Permit certainty, Sec. 1401: Lets an agency act in response to a federal court order or under any applicable law or regulation (was: only where a court required it). Nov. 7 p. 172, line 1
  • Disparate-treatment suits, Sec. 1402: Extends the comparison period for denial and delay data from 5 to 10 calendar years. Nov. 7 p. 182, line 14
  • Historic preservation and Tribes, Sec. 2301: Omits the September definition of “property of traditional religious or cultural importance”: a location or feature where an event of continuing religious or cultural significance to a living community occurred. Sept. 30 p. 372, line 14
  • Tribal preservation offices, Sec. 2301: Strikes the provision under which the Secretary of the Interior assumes the functions of a Tribal Historic Preservation Officer when a tribal program stays inconsistent after the 2-year period. Sept. 30 p. 377, line 10
  • Clean Water Act certification, Sec. 1202: Removes the requirement that conditions be consistent with the requirements of the Federal license or permit. Sept. 30 p. 105, line 17
  • Where NEPA suits are heard, Sec. 1110: Gives the D.C. Circuit original and exclusive jurisdiction over claims challenging an agency rule for NEPA compliance and claims challenging an authorization for a project with no physical location. Nov. 7 p. 74, line 1
  • Consolidated grid planning, Sec. 2106: Applies the consolidated planning rule to each Transmission Organization (was: each transmission planning region). Nov. 7 p. 278, line 7
  • Behind-the-meter resources, Sec. 2108: Qualifying behind-the-meter resources need a rated capacity of at least 5 kilowatts and no more than 5 megawatts (was: no upper limit). Nov. 7 p. 299, line 8
  • Help for communities weighing data centers, Sec. 2117: The Secretary of Energy must establish a Data Center Community Assistance Program within 180 days of enactment. Nov. 7 p. 341, line 21
  • Nuclear licensing hearings, Sec. 1501: The Nuclear Regulatory Commission may issue construction permits, operating licenses, combined licenses and amendments without a hearing. Nov. 7 p. 203, line 15
  • Brownfields funding, Sec. 1602: Appropriations of $200,000,000 for each of fiscal years 2027 through 2031 are authorized for the brownfields grant and loan program. Nov. 7 p. 227, line 9

The November 7 substitute

Sens. Capito, Lee, Whitehouse and Heinrich released an amendment in the nature of a substitute to H.R. 2347, the House-passed bill the Senate is using to carry the permitting bill. It replaces the House text with a revised BAAJA and keeps the House bill's tax provision as section 3001; the sponsors' release calls the changes clarifying, conforming and technical edits. 417 pages and 71 sections on September 30; 494 pages and 83 sections on November 7. 43 sections edited, 13 added, 1 dropped, 2 renumbered, 25 unchanged.

Division A, Title I: National Environmental Policy Act Of 1969

Sec. 1102. Definitions · Edited

  • Exempts from NEPA review agency actions that deploy broadband over wireline facilities on a previously analyzed right-of-way and are funded in whole or in part with Federal financial assistance. Nov. 7 p. 18, line 12
  • Adds a State or Tribal agency conducting an environmental review under Federal-law authority to the definition of lead agency. Nov. 7 p. 16, line 9
  • Excludes assistance under chapter 6 of title 23 or chapter 224 of title 49 from the exemption for actions whose only Federal involvement is a loan or loan guarantee. Nov. 7 p. 17, line 18
  • Treats the Department of Transportation as having predominant control over financial assistance for any project receiving more than $10,000,000 from it. Nov. 7 p. 23, line 7
  • States that the offshore exemption does not alter any requirement under the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act. Nov. 7 p. 19, line 16
  • Says the limit on capacity increases for lifeline infrastructure repairs does not apply to replacing existing fiber-optic cable, or to replacing copper or coaxial cable with fiber-optic cable. Nov. 7 p. 21, line 12

Sec. 1103. Implementing procedures · Edited

  • Adds guidance documents to the agency regulations, procedures and policies that agencies may not change under this section except as provided. Nov. 7 p. 28, line 8

Sec. 1106. Timely and unified Federal reviews · Edited

  • Adds the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia as an alternative venue for a project sponsor's petition over missed agency deadlines. Nov. 7 p. 59, line 5
  • Treats the Assistant Secretary of the Army for Civil Works as the head of the Corps of Engineers for the rule on agency heads whose reviews are late. Nov. 7 p. 58, line 9
  • Requires each agency to carry out its obligations under Federal law, including its comments on an applicable environmental document, concurrently with other reviews. Nov. 7 p. 45, line 15
  • Lets a lead agency tier to existing analysis, as well as adopt or incorporate it, including analysis prepared under Federal law or State and Tribal law. Nov. 7 p. 48, line 18
  • Assigns to the lead agency the Federal Register publication of the environmental impact statement notice. Nov. 7 p. 47, line 7
  • Replaces the term environmental review with preparation of an environmental document in several deadline and dispute provisions. Nov. 7 p. 53, line 20

Sec. 1109. Applications for authorizations · Edited

  • Keeps in force a shorter statutory deadline for finding an application complete. Nov. 7 p. 70, line 16
  • Starts review on receipt of a notice of staking or similar pre-permit notification. Nov. 7 p. 70, line 20
  • Changes the information-request timing reference from environmental review to preparation of an environmental document. Nov. 7 p. 71, line 1

Sec. 1110. Judicial review · Edited

  • Limits the review provision to claims where the public comment opportunity occurs after the date of enactment of the Act. Nov. 7 p. 72, line 18
  • Gives the D.C. Circuit original and exclusive jurisdiction over claims challenging an agency rule for NEPA compliance and claims challenging an authorization for a project with no physical location. Nov. 7 p. 74, line 1
  • Subjects the courts of appeals' jurisdiction to section 9(b) of the Natural Gas Act and section 313(b) of the Federal Power Act. Nov. 7 p. 73, line 8

Sec. 1121. Council on Environmental Quality · Edited

  • Adds the Federal Permitting Improvement Steering Council to the bodies consulted on the permitting portal and data standards. Nov. 7 p. 88, line 18
  • Replaces environmental review with environmental document in one notice reference. Nov. 7 p. 92, line 3

Sec. 1131. Definitions · Edited

  • Adds FAST Act definitions of covered project, Dashboard and Executive Director, adds environmental document to the NEPA terms and renumbers later paragraphs. Nov. 7 p. 102, line 3

Sec. 1132. Environmental Review and Permitting Tracker · Edited

  • Has the Federal Permitting Improvement Steering Council's Executive Director maintain the Tracker as a component of the Dashboard (was: the CEQ Chairman). Nov. 7 p. 103, line 9
  • Treats a FAST-41 covered project's Dashboard entry as its Tracker entry, so no agency or sponsor must submit duplicate information. Nov. 7 p. 107, line 4
  • Posts Tracker information on the Dashboard (was: the Council's website). Nov. 7 p. 106, line 16

Sec. 1134. Limitation on claims for FAST-41 covered projects · New

  • Shortens the filing period for judicial review of FAST-41 covered project authorizations to 150 days (was 2 years). Nov. 7 p. 108, line 24
  • Applies the shorter period to claims over authorizations whose notice of final agency action is published in the Federal Register on or after enactment. Nov. 7 p. 109, line 4

Division A, Title Ii: Clean Water Act

Sec. 1202. Clean Water Act permitting and State certification · Edited

  • Allows a certifying authority to deny certification if the application still lacks required information after notice to the applicant and a reasonable chance to supply missing materials. Nov. 7 p. 112, line 5
  • Removes the requirement that conditions be consistent with the requirements of the Federal license or permit. Sept. 30 p. 105, line 17
  • Permits a certifying authority, before December 31, 2027, to require additional application materials through one written notice to all applicants without promulgating regulations. Nov. 7 p. 118, line 3
  • Applies the consequence of failing to promulgate regulations only on or after December 31, 2027. Nov. 7 p. 117, line 18
  • Limits a Federal license or permit condition's override of certification conditions to cases where the certifying authority determines the conditions are necessary. Nov. 7 p. 112, line 24
  • Lets a point source discharge comply with water quality requirements or a waiver under this section. Nov. 7 p. 126, line 19

Sec. 1204. Permits for dredged or fill material · Edited

  • Adds Nationwide Permit 27 to the list of nationwide permits covered by the section. Nov. 7 p. 133, line 17
  • Rewrites the period-of-prohibition paragraph to say the Administrator may act under paragraph (1) only during the period that begins and ends as listed. Nov. 7 p. 131, line 10

Sec. 1205. Judicial review timeline clarity · Edited

  • Limits the 30-day public comment condition to comment periods that occur after the date of enactment of the Act. Nov. 7 p. 139, line 4

Division A, Title Iii: Endangered Species Act Of 1973

Sec. 1304. State assumption of consultation responsibilities · Edited

  • Requires the Secretary to terminate a State's participation in the program in the listed circumstances (was: the Secretary may). Nov. 7 p. 159, line 18
  • Lets a permit or license applicant elect to have the relevant Federal agency consult with the Secretary instead of the State (was: the applicant consults with the Secretary itself). Nov. 7 p. 155, line 25

Sec. 1305. Judicial review · Edited

  • Applies the venue rules in section 111 of NEPA and section 306108(c)(9)(E) of title 54 to a petition for review. Nov. 7 p. 162, line 22

Sec. 1308. National Fish and Wildlife Foundation Coastal State Fund · Edited

  • Redirects the Coastal State Fund (was: understanding and using coastal resources) to natural and nature-based infrastructure, habitat restoration and protection, and related capacity building and planning. Nov. 7 p. 165, line 16
  • Requires amounts above the 5 percent single-State cap to be reallocated among States that have not reached the cap. Nov. 7 p. 166, line 20
  • Has the Treasury Secretary distribute appropriated funds to the Foundation within 60 days; the funds stay available until expended. Nov. 7 p. 167, line 11

Sec. 1309. State wildlife action plans · New

  • The Secretary of the Interior must approve a State's wildlife conservation and restoration program within 180 days after the State submits its plan. Nov. 7 p. 167, line 23
  • On submission of a State's plan, the Secretary must conditionally authorize its program and set aside the State's amounts. Nov. 7 p. 168, line 8
  • A status report, with the reason, goes to the House Natural Resources and Senate Environment and Public Works Committees when a program is not approved in time. Nov. 7 p. 169, line 13

Division A, Title Iv: Project Certainty

Sec. 1401. Maintaining Federal authorizations or permits for projects with non-Federal sponsors · Edited

  • Lets an agency act in response to a federal court order or under any applicable law or regulation (was: only where a court required it). Nov. 7 p. 172, line 1
  • Drops the clear and convincing evidence standard for an agency to show that an exception applies. Sept. 30 p. 162, line 5
  • Adds “vacate,” “remand,” “reopen” and “materially” to the list of barred actions, and replaces full capacity with normal capacity. Nov. 7 p. 170, line 20
  • Names the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia as an additional venue for review of an agency action. Nov. 7 p. 173, line 9
  • Limits the 25 to 50 percent cost award to cases where Federal agencies interfered in bad faith. Nov. 7 p. 176, line 9
  • Rules out treating that cost recovery as relief when judging irreparable harm on a motion for temporary relief. Nov. 7 p. 177, line 23
  • Bars any payment resolving a dispute over a Federal permit unless a federal court issues a final judgment. Nov. 7 p. 179, line 15
  • A court-approved settlement or consent decree meeting listed findings counts as that final judgment. Nov. 7 p. 180, line 2

Sec. 1402. Ensuring fairness for specific types of covered projects · Edited

  • Changes the standard for relief from improper denial or delay to denial in bad faith, or delay, as part of a pattern of disparate treatment. Nov. 7 p. 188, line 5
  • Puts the burden on the Federal Government, once an applicant makes a prima facie case, to disprove it or show a lawful basis for the pattern. Nov. 7 p. 192, line 5
  • Extends the comparison period for denial and delay data from 5 to 10 calendar years. Nov. 7 p. 182, line 14
  • Adds the District of Columbia district court as an alternative venue for the cause of action. Nov. 7 p. 187, line 23
  • Bars any payment resolving a dispute over a Federal permit for a specific type of covered project unless a federal court issues a final judgment. Nov. 7 p. 196, line 20
  • A court-approved settlement or consent decree meeting listed findings counts as that final judgment. Nov. 7 p. 197, line 4
  • Removes subsection (c), the 1-year decision timeline for permits not subject to environmental review, which section 1403 now carries. Sept. 30 p. 173, line 4

Sec. 1403. Avoiding unreasonable delays in permitting · Edited

  • Moves the 1-year decision deadline from section 1402(c) into this section, for permits not requiring an environmental impact statement or environmental assessment. Nov. 7 p. 199, line 21
  • Measures the 1-year period from submission of the latest covered application (was: the date it was determined complete). Nov. 7 p. 199, line 23
  • Starts the 1-year period after a rehearing is complete, or after the deadline to seek rehearing expires, where Federal law provides for rehearing. Nov. 7 p. 200, line 5
  • Adds the District of Columbia district court as an alternative venue for a complaint over a missed deadline. Nov. 7 p. 200, line 21

Division A, Title V: Nuclear Energy Licensing

Sec. 1501. Updating Hearing Procedures · New

  • The Nuclear Regulatory Commission may issue construction permits, operating licenses, combined licenses and amendments without a hearing. Nov. 7 p. 203, line 15
  • It must give 30 days notice and publication in the Federal Register of its intent. Nov. 7 p. 203, line 21
  • Any hearing the Commission does hold under this subparagraph must use informal adjudicatory procedures. Nov. 7 p. 204, line 5
  • All applications and proceedings pending before the Commission on or after enactment fall under the section. Nov. 7 p. 204, line 24

Sec. 1502. Licensing of uranium enrichment facilities · New

  • Construction of a uranium enrichment facility may begin before its license is issued, on the same terms that apply to other fuel cycle facilities. Nov. 7 p. 205, line 10
  • An applicant must certify that it will notify the Governor, local governments and certain Tribes in writing at least 15 days before starting construction. Nov. 7 p. 206, line 7
  • The Commission keeps its authority to deny a license for failure to meet regulatory requirements, and the section does not affect NEPA, hearing rights or inspections. Nov. 7 p. 205, line 20
  • Within 180 days after enactment, the Commission must issue or revise regulations. Nov. 7 p. 207, line 1

Sec. 1503. Applicability of deadlines to certain nuclear facilities · New

  • Exempts authorizations for nuclear reactors and other facilities licensed under the Atomic Energy Act from certain NEPA environmental assessment and impact statement deadlines and the section 1403(b) permitting deadline. Nov. 7 p. 208, line 18
  • The exemption also covers authorizations issued under Atomic Energy Act sections 53, 63 or 81 and under parts 60 or 61 of title 10 of the Code of Federal Regulations. Nov. 7 p. 209, line 1

Sec. 1504. Compensation authority for career appointees · New

  • The Nuclear Regulatory Commission Chairman may set a career appointee's pay at up to 110 percent of the maximum basic pay for Senior Executive Service positions at the Commission. Nov. 7 p. 209, line 23
  • This authority applies regardless of when the career appointee was appointed and may not affect certification of the Commission's performance appraisal system. Nov. 7 p. 210, line 5
  • Each annual report on compensation authority must also state the number of career appointees whose pay is fixed under the new subsection. Nov. 7 p. 211, line 9

Division A, Title Vi: Other Environmental Matters

Sec. 1601. Wildfire emissions prevention · New

  • Prescribed fire counts as an exceptional event for Clean Air Act air quality monitoring data; the term includes cultural burning as determined by an Indian tribe. Nov. 7 p. 214, line 23
  • A State petition for an exceptional event is deemed approved if EPA does not act within 1 year, unless the Administrator later determines it is not one. Nov. 7 p. 218, line 12
  • Within 270 days of enactment, the Administrator must publish proposed revisions to the exceptional-event regulations and finalize them within 180 days after that. Nov. 7 p. 215, line 4
  • The Administrator may make competitive grants, with a federal share of up to 90 percent, for assessing and controlling wildfire smoke hazards in community buildings including schools. Nov. 7 p. 220, line 13
  • No more than 25 percent of each year's grant funds may go to recipients in one State. Nov. 7 p. 220, line 23

Sec. 1602. Brownfields revitalization · New

  • Appropriations of $200,000,000 for each of fiscal years 2027 through 2031 are authorized for the brownfields grant and loan program. Nov. 7 p. 227, line 9
  • Raises the remediation grant cap to $1,000,000 per site and the multipurpose grant cap from $1,000,000 to $2,000,000. Nov. 7 p. 221, line 14
  • Grant recipients may use up to 10 percent of the funds for demolition, subject to the EPA Administrator's approval. Nov. 7 p. 222, line 12
  • State and Tribal response program funding is authorized at $60 million in fiscal year 2027, rising by $5 million each year to $80 million in fiscal year 2031. Nov. 7 p. 228, line 7
  • Nonprofits described in section 501(c)(6) of the tax code become eligible entities for brownfields grants. Nov. 7 p. 221, line 7
  • The Administrator must waive the matching share requirement for entities in small communities or disadvantaged areas. Nov. 7 p. 226, line 23

Division B, Title I: Electric Transmission

Sec. 2101. Transmission permitting · Edited

  • Drops the provision that let FERC receive a permit application for facilities of 345 kilovolts or more at the same time as, or any time after, the state siting application. Sept. 30 p. 191, line 17
  • A transmitting utility, or an entity that will become one, must file a cost allocation tariff when FERC finds the permit criteria met. Nov. 7 p. 243, line 2
  • No permit needs to have been issued before that filing. Nov. 7 p. 243, line 7
  • Facilities below 230 kilovolts also qualify for a permit if they are necessary to safely and reliably interconnect or operate a qualifying facility for which the permit is provided. Nov. 7 p. 239, line 21
  • FERC must also find that the project will facilitate just and reasonable rates, in addition to protecting consumers and providing improved reliability. Nov. 7 p. 239, line 14
  • Replaces the wording that the facilities carry energy in interstate commerce, including from the outer Continental Shelf to a State, with interstate or foreign commerce. Sept. 30 p. 189, line 9
  • Tribal governments are listed with State and local governments as affected landowners. Nov. 7 p. 235, line 18
  • The ratepayer protection rule now covers customers of existing or proposed facilities whose costs are allocated under the cost allocation paragraph. Nov. 7 p. 244, line 18
  • Utilities may recover costs through voluntary agreement and allocate the remaining costs under the subsection. Nov. 7 p. 245, line 1

Sec. 2102. Reconductoring and other activities in existing rights-of-way · Edited

  • The additional area next to an existing right-of-way that counts for covered activities may extend no more than one-quarter mile from the right-of-way boundary. Nov. 7 p. 251, line 22
  • Covered transmission capacity activities and agency actions authorizing them are not a major Federal action under NEPA (was: a category excluded from environmental assessment or impact statement preparation). Nov. 7 p. 252, line 14
  • Adds installation of grid-enhancing advanced transmission technologies to the list of covered activities. Nov. 7 p. 253, line 10

Sec. 2103. Transmission planning · Edited

  • Exempt transmitting utilities now include a State or political subdivision, a Rural Electrification Act financed cooperative, or a cooperative selling under 4,000,000 megawatt hours a year. Nov. 7 p. 258, line 23
  • Agencies and wholly owned corporations of those entities are also covered (was: only a State or subdivision and a cooperative under that sales level). Nov. 7 p. 259, line 1
  • Rights, roles and opportunities given to States or their subdivisions in transmission planning must be given on a commensurate basis to Indian Tribes or their subdivisions. Nov. 7 p. 265, line 21
  • Planning must be conducted at least every 4 years, with the first planning cycle starting within 1 year after FERC approves the planning process (was: only updates every 4 years). Nov. 7 p. 266, line 10
  • A FERC order or rulemaking on transmission planning in effect at enactment counts as compliance with the section and relieves FERC of the requirements in subsection (c)(1). Nov. 7 p. 269, line 1
  • Cost allocation tariff filings are required of utilities that own, control, operate or propose to construct facilities selected in a regional or joint interregional plan. Nov. 7 p. 267, line 10
  • The September text covered facilities constructed or modified as a result of the section. Sept. 30 p. 215, line 20
  • States that, except as expressly provided, nothing in the section confers, limits or impairs FERC authority under any other law. Nov. 7 p. 270, line 9

Sec. 2104. Improvements relating to local transmission planning and development · Edited

  • Limits referrals to FERC for investigation of local transmission planning to State commissions with jurisdiction over the utility (was: States). Nov. 7 p. 272, line 7
  • The investigative team must issue a preliminary findings report within 120 days of the notice opening an investigation, and then a final report and recommendation to FERC. Nov. 7 p. 274, line 10
  • FERC must notice the final report for comment within 14 business days and issue an order within 120 days after that notice. Nov. 7 p. 275, line 7
  • Draws investigative team members from non-decisional individuals in an office designated by the FERC Chairman (was: individuals from the Office of Enforcement and Regulatory Accounting). Nov. 7 p. 273, line 20
  • Turns the fine of up to $5,000,000 per facility into a civil penalty credited to the transmission customers who bore the facility's costs (was: payable to the referring State). Nov. 7 p. 276, line 5
  • A local transmission facility must lie solely within a public utility transmission provider's retail distribution service territory and not be selected in a regional plan for cost allocation. Nov. 7 p. 271, line 4
  • Replacement of facilities is now covered. Nov. 7 p. 273, line 4
  • Orders under the section are subject to rehearing and judicial review under section 313; civil penalty orders follow section 31(d). Nov. 7 p. 277, line 5

Sec. 2105. Prohibition of right of first refusal · Edited

  • The federal right of first refusal ban for transmission facilities selected in a regional or interregional plan for cost allocation yields to FERC rules or orders in effect at enactment. Nov. 7 p. 277, line 16
  • September text applied the ban notwithstanding any such rule or order. Sept. 30 p. 224, line 9

Sec. 2106. Consolidated planning process · Edited

  • Applies the consolidated planning rule to each Transmission Organization (was: each transmission planning region). Nov. 7 p. 278, line 7
  • Planned interconnection locations for generating and storage capacity and for transmission capacity must be published for customers. Nov. 7 p. 278, line 16
  • An upfront, fixed, subregional per-megawatt fee must be published too (was: a zonal cost). Nov. 7 p. 278, line 22
  • The study process covers generator interconnection and transmission service requests. Nov. 7 p. 279, line 1
  • Withdrawal penalties apply after total interconnection costs and contingency limitations are given to the customer. Nov. 7 p. 279, line 7
  • Its aim is to co-optimize solutions that cost-effectively address electricity demand growth while integrating new generation and storage resources. Nov. 7 p. 279, line 12
  • FERC may find that current interconnection and planning processes comply with its rule, or that a Transmission Organization meets the rule's objectives through comparable processes. Nov. 7 p. 280, line 22
  • A process wins FERC approval if it meets the just and reasonable requirement of sections 205 and 206 and is consistent with the rule (was: consistent with the public interest). Nov. 7 p. 280, line 14

Sec. 2107. Ratepayer protection · Edited

  • Drops the September definition of covered load, which set a floor of 20 megawatts. Sept. 30 p. 232, line 9
  • Uses computational load sites with no megawatt floor and leaves the definition of an eligible customer to FERC by rule or order. Nov. 7 p. 283, line 9
  • FERC must revise its 1994 transmission pricing policy statement and issue a notice of proposed rulemaking within 90 days of enactment. Nov. 7 p. 285, line 1
  • The September text allowed 270 days for the policy statement and a further 270 days after its publication for the proposed rule. Sept. 30 p. 229, line 15
  • Eligible computational load customers pay the embedded cost and a reasonable assessment, as determined by FERC, of the incremental cost of transmission service (was: the incremental cost itself). Nov. 7 p. 285, line 11
  • Lets FERC treat these customers as a customer class and assign some or all costs to the class instead of to individual customers. Nov. 7 p. 287, line 23
  • States may revise an existing arrangement to serve a computational load site, to the extent state law permits, to match the new rate rules from the revision's effective date. Nov. 7 p. 298, line 4
  • Adds a policy that computational load owners and operators bear the full cost of electric service and infrastructure built for them, whether or not they take the service requested. Nov. 7 p. 281, line 8

Sec. 2108. Dispatchable energy systems market access · Edited

  • Qualifying behind-the-meter resources need a rated capacity of at least 5 kilowatts and no more than 5 megawatts (was: no upper limit). Nov. 7 p. 299, line 8
  • Practices listed as unjust and unreasonable take effect 18 months after enactment. Nov. 7 p. 302, line 2
  • A Transmission Organization may set narrowly tailored performance rules for aggregations that join capacity markets but not energy markets. Nov. 7 p. 302, line 23
  • Bars metering, telemetry or equipment requirements that are more stringent than the statutory definitions (was: inconsistent with the definitions). Nov. 7 p. 302, line 7
  • The bar on requiring energy market participation as a precondition to capacity market participation now applies when energy participation is with compensation and capacity participation is on equal terms. Nov. 7 p. 302, line 12
  • Removes the September provision barring distribution utilities from purposefully using control of the distribution system to keep eligible resources out of wholesale markets. Sept. 30 p. 253, line 18
  • Only subsections (b) and (c) fall under the voluntary-compliance exception. Nov. 7 p. 312, line 20
  • Double compensation draws a penalty of three times the amount received (was: counted each day the compensation was knowingly retained). Sept. 30 p. 250, line 18

Sec. 2109. Grid-enhancing advanced transmission technologies · Edited (September 30: Sec. 2109. Advanced transmission technologies)

  • Covers grid-enhancing advanced transmission technologies, defined as dynamic line rating, advanced power flow control, transmission topology optimization and high-performance transmission conductors. Nov. 7 p. 319, line 16
  • The September text covered advanced transmission technology as defined in the Energy Policy Act of 2005. Sept. 30 p. 260, line 9
  • Public utilities must evaluate their systems for opportunities to deploy these technologies. Nov. 7 p. 320, line 3
  • They must use them on covered projects whenever the benefits would exceed the costs (was: no benefit-cost condition). Nov. 7 p. 320, line 11
  • A class of high-performance conductors that FERC determines is an economically reasonable alternative falls under the best-available standard, taking into account reliability, safety and affordability. Nov. 7 p. 319, line 3
  • FERC also presumes that the costs of using a grid-enhancing technology are an economically reasonable alternative to other technologies. Nov. 7 p. 321, line 2
  • Deployment follows general schedules set by FERC (was: FERC set a timeline for the deployment). Nov. 7 p. 322, line 17
  • Removes the September amendment adding the four technologies to the Energy Policy Act of 2005 definition of advanced transmission technology. Sept. 30 p. 264, line 22

Sec. 2110. Expediting generator interconnection queues · Edited

  • FERC must initiate and finalize a rulemaking at least once every 4 years on whether utilities are adequately deploying advanced computing technologies (was: reopen the earlier rulemaking). Nov. 7 p. 324, line 10

Sec. 2111. Grid data access · Edited

  • A transmission provider that fails to follow the grid data duties faces civil penalties under section 316A (was: a matter affecting reliable operation of the bulk-power system under section 215). Nov. 7 p. 329, line 4
  • Covered studies include studies or analyses under sections 224, 225, 226, 228, 229 or 232 of the Federal Power Act, in addition to interconnection and similar tariff studies. Nov. 7 p. 325, line 7
  • For the study-related duties in subsection (b), an entity legally responsible for a covered study counts as a transmission provider. Nov. 7 p. 326, line 8
  • Each transmission provider must obtain, assemble and maintain the grid data (was: and use it). Nov. 7 p. 326, line 17

Sec. 2112. Power marketing administrations · Edited

  • Excludes from the section any power marketing administration employee whose position, before March 27, 2025, was governed by a collective bargaining agreement covered by the two named federal pay laws. Nov. 7 p. 337, line 7
  • Chapter 71 of title 5 applies to all power marketing administration employees as it did on March 26, 2025. Nov. 7 p. 337, line 15
  • Collective bargaining agreements in effect on that date stay in force through their stated terms. Nov. 7 p. 337, line 21
  • Pay must be comparable to the electric industry in the locality of the administration (was: the public sectors of the electric industry and consumer-owned utilities). Nov. 7 p. 332, line 22
  • The authority applies except as otherwise provided in the section (was: notwithstanding any other law, rule, regulation or directive on Federal employee pay other than chapter 83 of title 5). Nov. 7 p. 331, line 15

Sec. 2113. FERC fee assessments · Edited

  • Renumbers the section as section 318 of the Federal Power Act and defines the Commission by a short reference instead of repeating its full name. Nov. 7 p. 338, line 3

Sec. 2114. Collection of data on computational load entities · Edited

  • Replaces “data center entity,” an end-user or group of co-located end-users whose primary purpose is housing computing equipment, with “computational load site.” Sept. 30 p. 279, line 4
  • A computational load site is one or more buildings or installations at a single location that primarily contain information technology infrastructure. Nov. 7 p. 338, line 22
  • Listed uses are software services, artificial intelligence, cryptocurrency mining and other computational activities at a data center. Nov. 7 p. 339, line 5
  • September text listed AI, cloud computing or data hosting, and cryptocurrency or other digital-asset computation. Sept. 30 p. 279, line 12
  • The Energy Information Administration must revise Form 861 or any successor form to separately include information from computational load sites (was: include load from data center entities). Nov. 7 p. 339, line 18

Sec. 2115. De novo review of civil penalties under the Natural Gas Act · New

  • Civil penalties assessed under section 22 of the Natural Gas Act are reviewed under the same procedures that apply to civil penalties under section 31(d) of the Federal Power Act. Nov. 7 p. 339, line 23

Sec. 2116. Transmission Organization governance · New

  • FERC has jurisdiction over the corporate governance of a Transmission Organization for purposes of Federal Power Act section 205. Nov. 7 p. 340, line 7
  • The same statement is added to section 206, covering FERC review of existing rates and practices. Nov. 7 p. 340, line 13

Sec. 2117. Data Center Community Assistance Program · New

  • The Secretary of Energy must establish a Data Center Community Assistance Program within 180 days of enactment. Nov. 7 p. 341, line 21
