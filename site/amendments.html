Key changes

  • Data center costs, Sec. 2107: Drops the 20-megawatt floor for data center load by replacing “covered load” with computational load owners, operators and sites. Was: rules applied only to load of 20 megawatts or more. Sept. 30 p. 232, line 9
  • Permit certainty, Sec. 1401: Lets agencies act on any federal court order or under any applicable law. Was: only where a court required it. Nov. 7 p. 172, line 1
  • Disparate-treatment suits, Sec. 1402: Counts the 10 most recent calendar years of denial and delay data. Was: the 5 most recent. Nov. 7 p. 182, line 14
  • Historic preservation and Tribes, Sec. 2301: Omits the definition of “property of traditional religious or cultural importance” in section 302706(a). Was: a place where an event of continuing religious or cultural significance to a living community occurred. Sept. 30 p. 372, line 14
  • Tribal preservation offices, Sec. 2301: Drops the rule that the Secretary assumes the functions of a Tribal Historic Preservation Officer when a tribal program stays inconsistent after 2 years. Was: the Secretary took over all or part of those functions. Sept. 30 p. 377, line 10
  • Clean Water Act certification, Sec. 1202: Drops consistency with the Federal license or permit from the tests certification conditions must meet. Was: a third test, alongside necessity and least burden. Sept. 30 p. 105, line 17
  • Where NEPA suits are heard, Sec. 1110: Sends claims challenging a federal agency's NEPA rule, or an authorization for a project with no physical location, only to the D.C. Circuit. Was: the circuit where the project would be built or run. Nov. 7 p. 74, line 1
  • Consolidated grid planning, Sec. 2106: Applies the consolidated planning rule to each Transmission Organization. Was: each transmission planning region. Nov. 7 p. 278, line 7
  • Behind-the-meter resources, Sec. 2108: Limits qualifying behind-the-meter resources to a rated capacity of 5 kilowatts to 5 megawatts. Was: 5 kilowatts or more, with no upper limit. Nov. 7 p. 299, line 8
  • Help for communities weighing data centers, Sec. 2117: Directs the Secretary of Energy to set up a Data Center Community Assistance Program within 180 days of enactment. Nov. 7 p. 341, line 21
  • Nuclear licensing hearings, Sec. 1501: Lets the Nuclear Regulatory Commission issue construction permits, operating licenses, combined licenses and amendments without a hearing. Nov. 7 p. 203, line 15
  • Brownfields funding, Sec. 1602: Authorizes $200,000,000 for each of fiscal years 2027 through 2031 for the brownfields grant and loan program. Nov. 7 p. 227, line 9

The November 7 substitute

The four sponsors' revised text of BAAJA, offered as a substitute for H.R. 2347, the House bill the Senate is using to carry it. Substitute PDF · Sponsors' release · Sponsors' staff summary of changes

417 pages and 71 sections on September 30; 494 pages and 83 sections on November 7. 43 sections edited, 13 added, 1 dropped, 2 renumbered, 25 unchanged.

Division A, Title I: National Environmental Policy Act Of 1969

Sec. 1102. Definitions · Edited

  • Exempts wireline broadband projects on a previously analyzed right-of-way that get Federal funding from NEPA review. Was: no such exemption. Nov. 7 p. 18, line 12
  • Adds a State or Tribal agency that reviews the action under Federal-law authority to the lead agency definition. Was: Federal agencies only. Nov. 7 p. 16, line 9
  • Excludes assistance under chapter 6 of title 23 or chapter 224 of title 49 from the loan and loan guarantee exemption. Was: every loan or loan guarantee qualified. Nov. 7 p. 17, line 18
  • Treats the Department of Transportation as controlling use of financial assistance for any project it gives more than $10,000,000. Was: no such rule. Nov. 7 p. 23, line 7
  • Conditions the exemption for activities outside U.S. territory on leaving Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act requirements unchanged. Was: no such condition. Nov. 7 p. 19, line 16
  • Lifts the 20 percent capacity limit for lifeline wireline communications repairs that replace fiber-optic cable, or copper or coaxial cable with fiber. Was: the limit applied to every lifeline repair. Nov. 7 p. 21, line 12

Sec. 1103. Implementing procedures · Edited

  • Adds guidance documents to the agency materials that cannot be changed under this section. Was: only regulations, procedures and policies. Nov. 7 p. 28, line 8

Sec. 1106. Timely and unified Federal reviews · Edited

  • Adds the D.C. federal district court as a venue for missed-deadline suits. Was: only the district where the project sits. Nov. 7 p. 59, line 5
  • Counts the Assistant Secretary of the Army for Civil Works as head of the Corps of Engineers under the rule barring late agency heads from traveling over 25 miles. Was: no such designation. Nov. 7 p. 58, line 9
  • Lists an agency's legally required comments on an environmental document among the obligations to run concurrently with other reviews. Was: obligations unspecified. Nov. 7 p. 45, line 15
  • Lets a lead agency tier to existing analysis, and use analysis prepared under Federal law. Was: only adopt or incorporate analysis prepared under State or Tribal law. Nov. 7 p. 48, line 18
  • Assigns the lead agency to publish the environmental impact statement notice of intent in the Federal Register. Was: no publisher named. Nov. 7 p. 47, line 7
  • Extends the interagency dispute provisions to a Federal agency's required comments, and calls the covered step preparing an environmental document. Was: disputes over an environmental review or authorization. Nov. 7 p. 53, line 20

Sec. 1109. Applications for authorizations · Edited

  • Adds a paragraph keeping any shorter period other law sets for finding an application complete, and starting reviews on receipt of a notice of staking. Was: no such paragraph. Nov. 7 p. 70, line 16
  • Ties the application-information requirement to actions needing preparation of an environmental document. Was: actions requiring an environmental review. Nov. 7 p. 71, line 1

Sec. 1110. Judicial review · Edited

  • Requires a claimant to have submitted a substantive comment only if the comment opportunity came after the Act's enactment. Was: required whenever a comment opportunity was provided. Nov. 7 p. 72, line 18
  • Sends claims challenging a federal agency's NEPA rule, or an authorization for a project with no physical location, only to the D.C. Circuit. Was: the circuit where the project would be built or run. Nov. 7 p. 74, line 1
  • Lets section 9(b) of the Natural Gas Act and section 313(b) of the Federal Power Act override the circuit rule for these claims. Was: no such exceptions. Nov. 7 p. 73, line 8

Sec. 1121. Council on Environmental Quality · Edited

  • Adds the Federal Permitting Improvement Steering Council to the bodies consulted on permitting portal design and data standards. Was: not on the lists. Nov. 7 p. 88, line 18
  • Words the notice to local communities as one of intent to “prepare an environmental document”. Was: “begin an environmental review”. Nov. 7 p. 92, line 3

Sec. 1131. Definitions · Edited

  • Adds definitions of covered project, Dashboard and Executive Director from the FAST Act, plus “environmental document” among the NEPA terms. Was: none of the four terms defined. Nov. 7 p. 102, line 3

Sec. 1132. Environmental Review and Permitting Tracker · Edited

  • Makes the Executive Director of the Federal Permitting Improvement Steering Council maintain the Tracker as part of the Dashboard. Was: the CEQ Chairman maintained it, with the General Services Administration. Nov. 7 p. 103, line 9
  • Counts a FAST-41 covered project's Dashboard entry as its Tracker entry, so no agency or sponsor submits duplicate information. Was: no such rule. Nov. 7 p. 107, line 4
  • Posts Tracker updates on the Dashboard within 7 days. Was: on the Council's website. Nov. 7 p. 106, line 16

Sec. 1134. Limitation on claims for FAST-41 covered projects · New

  • Cuts the filing period for judicial review of FAST-41 covered project authorizations from 2 years to 150 days. Nov. 7 p. 108, line 24
  • Applies the 150-day period to claims over authorizations whose notice of final agency action appears in the Federal Register on or after enactment. Nov. 7 p. 109, line 4

Division A, Title Ii: Clean Water Act

Sec. 1202. Clean Water Act permitting and State certification · Edited

  • Permits denial when an application still lacks required information after notice and a chance to supply it. Was: denial only on clear and convincing evidence that no condition could prevent a violation. Nov. 7 p. 112, line 5
  • Drops consistency with the Federal license or permit from the tests certification conditions must meet. Was: a third test, alongside necessity and least burden. Sept. 30 p. 105, line 17
  • Allows a certifying authority, before December 31, 2027, to require extra application materials by one written notice to all applicants. Was: extra materials only through regulations. Nov. 7 p. 118, line 3
  • Counts an application as complete with only the listed items, if the authority has not promulgated regulations, only from December 31, 2027. Was: applied immediately. Nov. 7 p. 117, line 18
  • Ties the savings clause to the certifying authority's own determination that conditions are necessary. Was: conditions that were necessary, with no one named to decide. Nov. 7 p. 112, line 24
  • Accepts a certifying authority's statement that a discharge has a waiver under this section, as well as compliance with water quality requirements. Was: compliance statements only. Nov. 7 p. 126, line 19

Sec. 1204. Permits for dredged or fill material · Edited

  • Adds Nationwide Permit 27 to the permits the Secretary must keep as nationwide general permits. Was: Permits 12, 14, 43, 57 and 58. Nov. 7 p. 133, line 17
  • Reworks the paragraph into a limit: the Administrator may act under paragraph (1) only between a complete application and permit issuance. Was: the same window, written as a period definition. Nov. 7 p. 131, line 10

Sec. 1205. Judicial review timeline clarity · Edited

  • Limits the rule that only parties who commented may sue to actions with a 30-day comment period that came after the Act's enactment. Was: any 30-day comment period. Nov. 7 p. 139, line 4

Division A, Title Iii: Endangered Species Act Of 1973

Sec. 1305. Judicial review · Edited

Sec. 1308. National Fish and Wildlife Foundation Coastal State Fund · Edited

  • Directs Coastal State Fund grants to programs that implement natural and nature-based infrastructure, habitat restoration and protection, capacity building and planning. Was: programs to understand and use coastal resources and ecosystem services. Nov. 7 p. 165, line 16
  • Sends amounts that push a second State over the 5 percent cap to States still under it. Was: excess reallocated equally among the remaining States, with no cap check. Nov. 7 p. 166, line 20
  • Requires the Treasury Secretary to give the Foundation appropriated funds within 60 days, available until expended. Was: no timing rule. Nov. 7 p. 167, line 11

Sec. 1309. State wildlife action plans · New

  • Gives the Secretary of the Interior 180 days after a State submits its plan to approve the State's wildlife conservation and restoration program. Nov. 7 p. 167, line 23
  • Has the Secretary conditionally authorize a State's program and set aside the State's amounts when its plan arrives. Nov. 7 p. 168, line 8
  • Sends a status report with the reason to the House Natural Resources and Senate Environment and Public Works Committees when a program is not approved in time. Nov. 7 p. 169, line 13

Division A, Title Iv: Project Certainty

Sec. 1401. Maintaining Federal authorizations or permits for projects with non-Federal sponsors · Edited

  • Lets agencies act on any federal court order or under any applicable law. Was: only where a court required it. Nov. 7 p. 172, line 1
  • Drops “clear and convincing evidence” from the agency's burden of proof for showing an exception applies. Was: the agency had to meet that standard. Sept. 30 p. 162, line 5
  • Adds vacating, remanding and reopening to the barred actions, limits the bar on amending to material changes, and protects normal capacity. Was: any amendment or alteration, and full capacity. Nov. 7 p. 170, line 20
  • Opens the D.C. federal district court as a venue for suits over an agency action. Was: only a district where the project is at least partly located. Nov. 7 p. 173, line 9
  • Limits the cost award of 25 to 50 percent of project costs to cases of Federal bad-faith interference. Was: part of relief whenever the agency failed its burden. Nov. 7 p. 176, line 9
  • Bars courts from counting available cost recovery when judging irreparable harm on a motion for temporary or preliminary relief. Was: no such rule. Nov. 7 p. 177, line 23
  • Prohibits payments to resolve a Federal permit dispute without a federal court's final judgment, which may be a court-approved settlement or consent decree meeting listed findings. Was: no such bar. Nov. 7 p. 179, line 15
  • Reaches stop-work orders issued within 2 years before the Act's enactment. Was: within 1 year. Nov. 7 p. 178, line 3

Sec. 1402. Ensuring fairness for specific types of covered projects · Edited

  • Narrows the denial ground to denial in bad faith as part of a pattern of disparate treatment. Was: improper denial due to such a pattern. Nov. 7 p. 188, line 5
  • Once an applicant makes a prima facie case, requires the Federal Government to disprove it or show a lawful basis for the pattern. Was: only a duty to produce raw statistical data. Nov. 7 p. 192, line 5
  • Counts the 10 most recent calendar years of denial and delay data. Was: the 5 most recent. Nov. 7 p. 182, line 14
  • Adds the D.C. federal district court as a venue for the cause of action. Was: only a district where the project is at least partly located. Nov. 7 p. 187, line 23
  • Bars payments to resolve a Federal permit dispute without a federal court's final judgment, which may be a court-approved settlement or consent decree meeting listed findings. Was: no such bar. Nov. 7 p. 196, line 20
  • Moves the 1-year decision timeline for permits without environmental review to section 1403(b). Was: subsection (c) of this section carried it. Sept. 30 p. 173, line 4

Sec. 1403. Avoiding unreasonable delays in permitting · Edited

  • Carries the 1-year decision deadline for permits that need no environmental impact statement or assessment. Was: section 1402(c) carried it. Nov. 7 p. 199, line 21
  • Runs the 1-year period from submission of the latest covered application. Was: the date it was determined complete. Nov. 7 p. 199, line 23
  • Starts the 1-year period the day after a rehearing ends, or after the deadline to seek one expires, where Federal law allows rehearing. Was: no rehearing rule. Nov. 7 p. 200, line 5
  • Adds the D.C. federal district court as a venue for suits over a missed deadline. Was: only a district where the project is at least partly located. Nov. 7 p. 200, line 21

Division A, Title V: Nuclear Energy Licensing

Sec. 1501. Updating Hearing Procedures · New

  • Lets the Nuclear Regulatory Commission issue construction permits, operating licenses, combined licenses and amendments without a hearing. Nov. 7 p. 203, line 15
  • Requires 30 days notice of its intent, published once in the Federal Register. Nov. 7 p. 203, line 21
  • Limits any hearing the Commission does hold under this subparagraph to informal adjudicatory procedures. Nov. 7 p. 204, line 5
  • Applies to all applications and proceedings pending before the Commission on or after enactment. Nov. 7 p. 204, line 24

Sec. 1502. Licensing of uranium enrichment facilities · New

  • Allows construction of a uranium enrichment facility to begin before its license is issued, on the same terms as other fuel cycle facilities. Nov. 7 p. 205, line 10
  • Requires an applicant to certify that it will notify the Governor, local governments and certain Tribes in writing at least 15 days before construction starts. Nov. 7 p. 206, line 7
  • Keeps the Commission's authority to deny a license for failure to meet regulatory requirements; NEPA, hearing rights and inspections are unaffected. Nov. 7 p. 205, line 20
  • Gives the Commission 180 days after enactment to issue or revise regulations. Nov. 7 p. 207, line 1

Sec. 1503. Applicability of deadlines to certain nuclear facilities · New

  • Exempts authorizations for nuclear reactors and other facilities licensed under the Atomic Energy Act from certain NEPA environmental assessment and impact statement deadlines and the section 1403(b) permitting deadline. Nov. 7 p. 208, line 18
  • Extends the exemption to authorizations issued under Atomic Energy Act sections 53, 63 or 81 and under parts 60 or 61 of title 10 of the Code of Federal Regulations. Nov. 7 p. 209, line 1

Sec. 1504. Compensation authority for career appointees · New

  • Lets the Nuclear Regulatory Commission Chairman set a career appointee's pay at up to 110 percent of the maximum basic pay for Senior Executive Service positions at the Commission. Nov. 7 p. 209, line 23
  • Covers a career appointee whenever appointed, and cannot affect certification of the Commission's performance appraisal system. Nov. 7 p. 210, line 5
  • Adds to each annual report on compensation authority the number of career appointees whose pay is fixed under the new subsection. Nov. 7 p. 211, line 9

  • Deems a State petition for an exceptional event approved if EPA does not act within 1 year, unless the Administrator later determines it is not one. Nov. 7 p. 218, line 12

  • Allows competitive grants with a federal share of up to 90 percent for assessing and controlling wildfire smoke hazards in community buildings, including schools. Nov. 7 p. 220, line 13
  • Caps at 25 percent the share of each year's grant funds going to recipients in one State. Nov. 7 p. 220, line 23

Sec. 1602. Brownfields revitalization · New

  • Authorizes $200,000,000 for each of fiscal years 2027 through 2031 for the brownfields grant and loan program. Nov. 7 p. 227, line 9
  • Raises the remediation grant cap to $1,000,000 per site and the multipurpose grant cap from $1,000,000 to $2,000,000. Nov. 7 p. 221, line 14
  • Lets grant recipients use up to 10 percent of the funds for demolition, with the EPA Administrator's approval. Nov. 7 p. 222, line 12
  • Sets State and Tribal response program authorizations at $60 million for fiscal year 2027, rising by $5 million each year to $80 million for fiscal year 2031. Nov. 7 p. 228, line 7
  • Adds nonprofits described in section 501(c)(6) of the tax code to the eligible entities for brownfields grants. Nov. 7 p. 221, line 7
  • Requires the Administrator to waive the matching share requirement for entities in small communities or disadvantaged areas. Nov. 7 p. 226, line 23

Division B, Title I: Electric Transmission

Sec. 2101. Transmission permitting · Edited

  • Removes the rule letting an application for lines of 345 kilovolts or more reach FERC at the same time as, or after, the state siting application. Was: allowed. Sept. 30 p. 191, line 17
  • Requires a utility, or an entity about to become one, to file a cost allocation tariff when FERC finds the permit criteria met. Was: only utilities that own, control or operate the facilities. Nov. 7 p. 243, line 2
  • Allows that tariff filing before any permit is issued. Was: no such rule. Nov. 7 p. 243, line 7
  • Qualifies lines below 230 kilovolts for a permit if needed to safely and reliably interconnect or operate a 230-kilovolt facility. Was: only lines of 230 kilovolts or more, or advanced conductors at FERC's voltage. Nov. 7 p. 239, line 21
  • Makes FERC also find that the project will facilitate just and reasonable rates. Was: consumer protection and reliability only. Nov. 7 p. 239, line 14
  • Drops “including transmission from the outer Continental Shelf to a State” from the commerce test. Was: that phrase followed “interstate commerce”. Sept. 30 p. 189, line 9
  • Adds Tribal governments to the State and local governments listed among affected landowners. Was: State and local governments only. Nov. 7 p. 235, line 18
  • Extends the rule against involuntary cost allocation to customers of existing or proposed facilities whose costs are allocated under the cost allocation paragraph. Was: facilities built or modified under this section. Nov. 7 p. 244, line 18
  • Lets utilities recover part of the costs by voluntary agreement and allocate the remaining costs under the subsection. Was: recovery by voluntary agreement only. Nov. 7 p. 245, line 1

Sec. 2102. Reconductoring and other activities in existing rights-of-way · Edited

  • Limits the added area next to an existing right-of-way that covered activities may use to one-quarter mile from the boundary. Was: no distance limit. Nov. 7 p. 251, line 22
  • Drops the categorical exclusion for covered capacity activities and the agency actions authorizing them; the not-a-major-Federal-action rule stays. Was: both applied. Sept. 30 p. 201, line 23
  • Adds installation of grid-enhancing advanced transmission technologies to the covered activities. Was: not listed. Nov. 7 p. 253, line 10

Sec. 2103. Transmission planning · Edited

  • Adds cooperatives financed under the Rural Electrification Act of 1936 to the exempt transmitting utilities. Was: only cooperatives selling under 4,000,000 megawatt hours a year. Nov. 7 p. 258, line 23
  • Covers agencies, authorities and instrumentalities of those entities, and corporations they wholly own, as exempt transmitting utilities. Was: a State or subdivision and small cooperatives only. Nov. 7 p. 259, line 1
  • Gives Indian Tribes and their subdivisions any right, role or opportunity States or their subdivisions get in transmission planning, on a commensurate basis. Was: no such rule. Nov. 7 p. 265, line 21
  • Runs planning at least every 4 years, with the first cycle starting within 1 year after FERC approves the process. Was: only updates every 4 years. Nov. 7 p. 266, line 10
  • Relieves FERC of the requirements in subsection (c)(1) where a planning order or rulemaking was already in effect at enactment. Was: such orders only counted as compliance with the planning and cost allocation requirements. Nov. 7 p. 269, line 1
  • Has utilities that own, operate or propose to build facilities selected in a regional or interregional plan file a cost allocation tariff. Was: facilities built or modified as a result of the section. Nov. 7 p. 267, line 10
  • Includes a savings clause: except as expressly provided, nothing in the section confers, limits or impairs FERC authority under other law. Was: no such clause. Nov. 7 p. 270, line 9

Sec. 2104. Improvements relating to local transmission planning and development · Edited

  • Limits referrals to FERC to State commissions with jurisdiction over the utility. Was: States. Nov. 7 p. 272, line 7
  • Requires the investigative team to issue a preliminary findings report within 120 days of the notice opening an investigation, then a final report and recommendation. Was: one preliminary findings report, no deadline. Nov. 7 p. 274, line 10
  • Sets 14 business days for FERC to notice the final report for comment, and 120 days after that to issue an order. Was: no deadlines. Nov. 7 p. 275, line 7
  • Draws investigative team members from non-decisional individuals in an office the FERC Chairman designates. Was: individuals from the Office of Enforcement and Regulatory Accounting. Nov. 7 p. 273, line 20
  • Makes the fine of up to $5,000,000 per facility a civil penalty credited to transmission customers who bore the facility's costs. Was: payable to the referring State. Nov. 7 p. 276, line 5
  • Defines local facilities as lying solely within a utility's retail distribution territory and not selected in a regional plan for cost allocation. Was: within one utility's territory, with costs recovered entirely from its customers. Nov. 7 p. 271, line 4
  • Adds replacement of local transmission facilities to the processes FERC can examine. Was: planning and development only. Nov. 7 p. 273, line 4
  • Subjects Commission orders under the section to rehearing and judicial review under section 313, with civil penalty orders following section 31(d). Was: no review provision. Nov. 7 p. 277, line 5

Sec. 2105. Prohibition of right of first refusal · Edited

  • Lets any FERC rule or order in effect at enactment override the federal ban on a right of first refusal for planned transmission facilities. Was: the ban applied despite any such rule or order. Nov. 7 p. 277, line 16

Sec. 2106. Consolidated planning process · Edited

  • Applies the consolidated planning rule to each Transmission Organization. Was: each transmission planning region. Nov. 7 p. 278, line 7
  • Lists storage capacity, and transmission capacity for customers, among the planned interconnection locations the rule must publish. Was: generating capacity only. Nov. 7 p. 278, line 16
  • Makes the upfront, fixed per-megawatt charge a subregional fee for each planned generation and storage location. Was: a zonal cost for each planned interconnection location. Nov. 7 p. 278, line 22
  • Extends the study process from generator clusters to interconnection and transmission service requests. Was: a generator cluster study process. Nov. 7 p. 279, line 1
  • Starts withdrawal penalties only after the customer receives total interconnection costs and contingency limits. Was: penalties with no stated trigger. Nov. 7 p. 279, line 7
  • Adds an aim to co-optimize solutions that cost-effectively address demand growth while integrating new generation and storage. Was: no such aim. Nov. 7 p. 279, line 12
  • Lets FERC find that current interconnection and planning processes comply with the rule, or that a Transmission Organization meets its objectives through comparable processes. Was: no such provision. Nov. 7 p. 280, line 22
  • Tests a process for consistency with the rule under subsection (a). Was: consistency with the public interest. Nov. 7 p. 280, line 14

Sec. 2107. Ratepayer protection · Edited

  • Drops the 20-megawatt floor for data center load by replacing “covered load” with computational load owners, operators and sites. Was: rules applied only to load of 20 megawatts or more. Sept. 30 p. 232, line 9
  • Leaves the definition of an eligible computational load customer to FERC by rule or order. Was: no such delegation. Nov. 7 p. 284, line 15
  • Gives FERC 90 days after enactment to revise the 1994 transmission pricing policy statement and issue a proposed rule. Was: 270 days for the statement, then 270 days from publication for the rule. Nov. 7 p. 285, line 1
  • Charges eligible computational load customers the embedded cost and a reasonable assessment, set by FERC, of the incremental cost of transmission service. Was: the incremental cost itself. Nov. 7 p. 285, line 11
  • Lets FERC treat these customers as a customer class and assign some or all costs to the class. Was: no class treatment. Nov. 7 p. 287, line 23
  • Permits a State, to the extent State law allows, to revise an existing arrangement to match the new rate rules from the revision's effective date. Was: existing arrangements remained in effect. Nov. 7 p. 298, line 4
  • Adds a policy that computational load owners and operators bear the full cost of service and infrastructure built for them. Was: no such policy. Nov. 7 p. 281, line 8

Sec. 2108. Dispatchable energy systems market access · Edited

  • Limits qualifying behind-the-meter resources to a rated capacity of 5 kilowatts to 5 megawatts. Was: 5 kilowatts or more, with no upper limit. Nov. 7 p. 299, line 8
  • Delays the practices listed as unjust and unreasonable until 18 months after enactment. Was: no delay. Nov. 7 p. 302, line 2
  • Lets a Transmission Organization set narrowly tailored performance rules for aggregations that join capacity markets but not energy markets. Was: no such allowance. Nov. 7 p. 302, line 23
  • Bars metering, telemetry or equipment requirements more stringent than the statutory definitions. Was: barred requirements inconsistent with the definitions. Nov. 7 p. 302, line 7
  • Narrows the bar on requiring energy market participation before capacity market participation to compensated participation and equal terms. Was: any such precondition. Nov. 7 p. 302, line 12
  • Drops the prohibition on distribution utilities purposefully blocking eligible resources from wholesale markets. Was: a prohibition on using control of the distribution system or retail sales to do so. Sept. 30 p. 253, line 18
  • Confines the voluntary-compliance exception to subsections (b) and (c). Was: subsections (b), (c) and (d). Nov. 7 p. 312, line 20
  • Sets the penalty for double compensation at 3 times the amount received. Was: 3 times the amount for each day it was knowingly retained. Sept. 30 p. 250, line 18

Sec. 2109. Grid-enhancing advanced transmission technologies · Edited (September 30: Sec. 2109. Advanced transmission technologies)

  • Defines grid-enhancing advanced transmission technology as dynamic line rating, advanced power flow control, transmission topology optimization and high-performance conductors. Was: advanced transmission technology as defined in the Energy Policy Act of 2005. Nov. 7 p. 319, line 16
  • Requires public utilities to evaluate their transmission systems for chances to deploy these technologies. Was: subsection (b) required only use on covered projects. Nov. 7 p. 320, line 3
  • Conditions use on covered projects on benefits exceeding costs. Was: use required with no benefit-cost condition. Nov. 7 p. 320, line 11
  • Makes the best-available standard a class of high-performance conductors that FERC finds economically reasonable, considering reliability, safety and affordability. Was: an individual conductor with the best feasible performance. Nov. 7 p. 319, line 3
  • Adds a presumption that the costs of using a grid-enhancing technology are an economically reasonable alternative to other technologies. Was: presumptions of prudence and just and reasonable costs only. Nov. 7 p. 321, line 2
  • Sets deployment on general schedules determined by FERC. Was: a timeline determined by FERC. Nov. 7 p. 322, line 17
  • Drops the amendment adding the four technologies to the Energy Policy Act of 2005 definition of advanced transmission technology. Was: the four technologies were added to that definition. Sept. 30 p. 264, line 22

Sec. 2110. Expediting generator interconnection queues · Edited

  • Requires each transmission provider only to obtain, assemble and maintain grid data. Was: obtain, assemble, maintain and use it. Nov. 7 p. 326, line 17
