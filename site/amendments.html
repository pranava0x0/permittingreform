Amendments to the permitting bill

What the November 7 substitute changes in each section of the September 30 text, with page and line cites in both PDFs, other amendments filed or announced, and who has said they support or oppose them.

Key changes

  • Data center costs, Sec. 2107: The substitute drops the September definition of covered load, which set a floor of 20 megawatts; it uses computational load sites with no megawatt floor and leaves the definition of an eligible customer to FERC by rule or order. Sept. 30 p. 232, line 9
  • Permit certainty, Sec. 1401: The substitute lets an agency act in response to a federal court order or under any applicable law or regulation; the September text allowed action only where a court required it. Nov. 7 p. 172, line 1
  • Disparate-treatment suits, Sec. 1402: The substitute extends the comparison period for denial and delay data from 5 to 10 calendar years. Nov. 7 p. 182, line 14
  • Historic preservation and Tribes, Sec. 2301: The substitute omits the September amendment to 54 U.S.C. 302706 that defined “property of traditional religious or cultural importance” as a geographic location or feature where an event of continuing religious or cultural significance to a living community occurred. Sept. 30 p. 372, line 14
  • Tribal preservation offices, Sec. 2301: The substitute strikes the September provision under which the Secretary of the Interior assumes the functions of a Tribal Historic Preservation Officer when a tribal program stays inconsistent after the 2-year period. Sept. 30 p. 377, line 10
  • Clean Water Act certification, Sec. 1202: The substitute removes the requirement that conditions be consistent with the requirements of the Federal license or permit. Sept. 30 p. 105, line 17
  • Where NEPA suits are heard, Sec. 1110: The substitute gives the D.C. Circuit original and exclusive jurisdiction over claims challenging an agency rule for NEPA compliance and claims challenging an authorization for a project with no physical location. Nov. 7 p. 74, line 1
  • Consolidated grid planning, Sec. 2106: The consolidated planning rule applies to each Transmission Organization, where the September text applied to each transmission planning region. Nov. 7 p. 278, line 7
  • Behind-the-meter resources, Sec. 2108: A qualifying behind-the-meter resource must have a rated capacity of at least 5 kilowatts and no more than 5 megawatts, where the September text set no upper limit. Nov. 7 p. 299, line 8
  • Help for communities weighing data centers, Sec. 2117: The Secretary of Energy must establish a Data Center Community Assistance Program within 180 days of enactment to give State, local and Tribal governments, on request, information and technical assistance on the energy, water, infrastructure and economic impacts of computational load site development. Nov. 7 p. 341, line 21
  • Nuclear licensing hearings, Sec. 1501: The Nuclear Regulatory Commission may issue construction permits, operating licenses, combined licenses and amendments without a hearing when no one whose interest may be affected requests one, after 30 days notice in the Federal Register. Nov. 7 p. 203, line 15
  • Brownfields funding, Sec. 1602: Appropriations of $200,000,000 for each of fiscal years 2027 through 2031 are authorized for the brownfields grant and loan program. Nov. 7 p. 227, line 9

The November 7 substitute

Sens. Capito, Lee, Whitehouse and Heinrich released an amendment in the nature of a substitute to H.R. 2347, the House-passed bill the Senate is using to carry the permitting bill. It replaces the House text with a revised BAAJA and keeps the House bill's tax provision as section 3001; the sponsors' release calls the changes clarifying, conforming and technical edits. Substitute PDF · Sponsors' release · Sponsors' staff summary of changes

417 pages and 71 sections on September 30; 494 pages and 83 sections on November 7. 43 sections edited, 13 added, 1 dropped, 2 renumbered, 25 unchanged.

Division A, Title I: National Environmental Policy Act Of 1969

Sec. 1102. Definitions · Edited

  • The substitute exempts from NEPA review any agency action for deploying broadband over wireline facilities on a previously analyzed right-of-way that is funded in whole or in part with Federal financial assistance. Nov. 7 p. 18, line 12
  • The substitute adds a State or Tribal government agency conducting an environmental review under authority granted by Federal law to the definition of lead agency. Nov. 7 p. 16, line 9
  • The substitute excludes assistance under chapter 6 of title 23 or chapter 224 of title 49 from the exemption for actions whose only Federal involvement is a loan or loan guarantee. Nov. 7 p. 17, line 18
  • The substitute adds a rule that the Department of Transportation is treated as having predominant control over financial assistance for any project receiving more than $10,000,000 from it. Nov. 7 p. 23, line 7
  • The substitute states that the offshore exemption does not alter any requirement under the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act. Nov. 7 p. 19, line 16
  • The substitute says the limit on capacity increases for lifeline infrastructure repairs does not apply to replacing existing fiber-optic cable, or replacing copper or coaxial cable with fiber-optic cable. Nov. 7 p. 21, line 12

Sec. 1103. Implementing procedures · Edited

  • The substitute adds guidance documents to the list of existing agency regulations, procedures, and policies that agencies may not change under this section except as provided. Nov. 7 p. 28, line 8

Sec. 1106. Timely and unified Federal reviews · Edited

  • The substitute adds the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia as an alternative venue for a project sponsor's petition over agency failures to meet deadlines. Nov. 7 p. 59, line 5
  • The substitute treats the Assistant Secretary of the Army for Civil Works as the head of the Corps of Engineers for the rule on agency heads whose reviews are late. Nov. 7 p. 58, line 9
  • The substitute requires each agency to carry out its obligations under Federal law, including its comments on an applicable environmental document, concurrently with other reviews. Nov. 7 p. 45, line 15
  • The substitute lets a lead agency tier to, as well as adopt or incorporate, existing analysis, including analysis prepared under Federal law or State and Tribal law. Nov. 7 p. 48, line 18
  • The substitute requires that an environmental impact statement notice in the Federal Register be published by the lead agency. Nov. 7 p. 47, line 7
  • The substitute replaces the term environmental review with preparation of an environmental document in several deadline and dispute provisions. Nov. 7 p. 53, line 20

Sec. 1109. Applications for authorizations · Edited

  • The substitute adds that a shorter statutory deadline for finding an application complete stays in force, and that review starts on receipt of a notice of staking or similar pre-permit notification. Nov. 7 p. 70, line 16
  • The substitute changes the information-request timing reference from environmental review to preparation of an environmental document. Nov. 7 p. 71, line 1

Sec. 1110. Judicial review · Edited

  • The substitute limits the review provision to claims where the public comment opportunity occurs after the date of enactment of the Act. Nov. 7 p. 72, line 18
  • The substitute gives the D.C. Circuit original and exclusive jurisdiction over claims challenging an agency rule for NEPA compliance and claims challenging an authorization for a project with no physical location. Nov. 7 p. 74, line 1
  • The substitute makes the courts of appeals' jurisdiction subject to section 9(b) of the Natural Gas Act and section 313(b) of the Federal Power Act. Nov. 7 p. 73, line 8

Sec. 1121. Council on Environmental Quality · Edited

  • The substitute adds the Federal Permitting Improvement Steering Council to the bodies consulted on the permitting portal and data standards, and replaces environmental review with environmental document in one notice reference. Nov. 7 p. 88, line 18

Sec. 1131. Definitions · Edited

  • The substitute adds FAST Act definitions of covered project, Dashboard, and Executive Director, adds environmental document to the NEPA terms, and renumbers later paragraphs. Nov. 7 p. 102, line 3

Sec. 1132. Environmental Review and Permitting Tracker · Edited

  • The substitute has the Federal Permitting Improvement Steering Council's Executive Director, rather than the CEQ Chairman, maintain the Tracker as a component of the Dashboard. Nov. 7 p. 103, line 9
  • The substitute makes a FAST-41 covered project's Dashboard entry its Tracker entry, and that no agency or sponsor must submit duplicate information. Nov. 7 p. 107, line 4
  • The substitute has the Tracker's information posted on the Dashboard instead of the Council's website. Nov. 7 p. 106, line 16

Sec. 1134. Limitation on claims for FAST-41 covered projects · New

  • The substitute shortens the filing period for judicial review claims over FAST-41 covered project authorizations from 2 years to 150 days. Nov. 7 p. 108, line 24
  • The shorter period applies to claims over authorizations whose notice of final agency action is published in the Federal Register on or after enactment. Nov. 7 p. 109, line 4

Division A, Title Ii: Clean Water Act

Sec. 1202. Clean Water Act permitting and State certification · Edited

  • The substitute lets a certifying authority deny certification if, after notifying the applicant and giving a reasonable opportunity to supply missing materials, the application still lacks required information. Nov. 7 p. 112, line 5
  • The substitute removes the requirement that conditions be consistent with the requirements of the Federal license or permit. Sept. 30 p. 105, line 17
  • The substitute allows a certifying authority, before December 31, 2027, to require additional application materials through one written notice to all applicants without promulgating regulations. Nov. 7 p. 118, line 3
  • The substitute applies the consequence of failing to promulgate regulations only on or after December 31, 2027. Nov. 7 p. 117, line 18
  • The substitute allows a Federal license or permit condition to override certification conditions only if the certifying authority determines the conditions are necessary. Nov. 7 p. 112, line 24
  • The substitute allows a point source discharge to comply with water quality requirements or a waiver under this section. Nov. 7 p. 126, line 19

Sec. 1204. Permits for dredged or fill material · Edited

  • The substitute adds Nationwide Permit 27 to the list of nationwide permits covered by the section. Nov. 7 p. 133, line 17
  • The substitute rewrites the period-of-prohibition paragraph to say the Administrator may act under paragraph (1) only during the period that begins and ends as listed. Nov. 7 p. 131, line 10

Sec. 1205. Judicial review timeline clarity · Edited

  • The substitute limits the 30-day public comment condition to comment periods that occur after the date of enactment of the Act. Nov. 7 p. 139, line 4

Division A, Title Iii: Endangered Species Act Of 1973

Sec. 1304. State assumption of consultation responsibilities · Edited

  • The substitute requires the Secretary to terminate a State's participation in the program in the listed circumstances, where the September text said the Secretary may. Nov. 7 p. 159, line 18
  • The substitute lets a permit or license applicant elect to have the relevant Federal agency consult with the Secretary rather than with the State, where the September text let the applicant elect to consult with the Secretary itself. Nov. 7 p. 155, line 25

Sec. 1305. Judicial review · Edited

  • Under the substitute, a petition for review follows the venue rules in section 111 of NEPA and section 306108(c)(9)(E) of title 54. Nov. 7 p. 162, line 22

Sec. 1308. National Fish and Wildlife Foundation Coastal State Fund · Edited

  • The substitute redirects the Coastal State Fund from understanding and using coastal resources to natural and nature-based infrastructure, habitat restoration and protection, and related capacity building and planning. Nov. 7 p. 165, line 16
  • The substitute requires amounts above the 5 percent single-State cap to be reallocated among States that have not reached the cap. Nov. 7 p. 166, line 20
  • The substitute requires the Treasury Secretary to distribute appropriated funds to the Foundation within 60 days, and makes the funds available until expended. Nov. 7 p. 167, line 11

Sec. 1309. State wildlife action plans · New

  • The section requires the Secretary of the Interior to approve a State's wildlife conservation and restoration program within 180 days after the State submits its plan. Nov. 7 p. 167, line 23
  • The section requires the Secretary to conditionally authorize a State's program on submission of its plan and to set aside the State's amounts. Nov. 7 p. 168, line 8
  • The section requires a status report to the House Natural Resources and Senate Environment and Public Works Committees when a program is not approved in time, including the reason. Nov. 7 p. 169, line 13

Division A, Title Iv: Project Certainty

Sec. 1401. Maintaining Federal authorizations or permits for projects with non-Federal sponsors · Edited

  • The substitute lets an agency act in response to a federal court order or under any applicable law or regulation; the September text allowed action only where a court required it. Nov. 7 p. 172, line 1
  • The substitute removes the clear and convincing evidence standard, so the agency must demonstrate that an exception applies without a stated heightened standard. Sept. 30 p. 162, line 5
  • The substitute adds vacate, remand, reopen, and materially as terms in the list of barred actions, and replaces full capacity with normal capacity. Nov. 7 p. 170, line 20
  • The substitute makes the District of Columbia district court an additional venue for review of an agency action. Nov. 7 p. 173, line 9
  • The substitute limits the 25 to 50 percent cost award to cases where Federal agencies interfered in bad faith, and bars treating that cost recovery as relief when judging irreparable harm on a motion for temporary relief. Nov. 7 p. 176, line 9
  • The substitute bars any payment resolving a dispute over a Federal permit unless a federal court issues a final judgment, which can be a court-approved settlement or consent decree meeting listed findings. Nov. 7 p. 179, line 15

Sec. 1402. Ensuring fairness for specific types of covered projects · Edited

  • The substitute changes the standard for relief from improper denial or delay to denial in bad faith, or delay, as part of a pattern of disparate treatment. Nov. 7 p. 188, line 5
  • The substitute adds that, once an applicant makes a prima facie case, the Federal Government bears the burden of disproving it or showing a lawful basis for the pattern. Nov. 7 p. 192, line 5
  • The substitute extends the comparison period for denial and delay data from 5 to 10 calendar years. Nov. 7 p. 182, line 14
  • The substitute adds the District of Columbia district court as an alternative venue for the cause of action. Nov. 7 p. 187, line 23
  • The substitute bars any payment to resolve a dispute over a Federal permit for a specific type of covered project without a final federal court judgment, including a court-approved settlement or consent decree meeting listed findings. Nov. 7 p. 196, line 20
  • The substitute removes subsection (c), the 1-year decision timeline for permits not subject to environmental review, which section 1403 now carries. Sept. 30 p. 173, line 4

Sec. 1403. Avoiding unreasonable delays in permitting · Edited

  • The substitute moves the 1-year decision deadline from section 1402(c) into this section, for permits not requiring an environmental impact statement or environmental assessment. Nov. 7 p. 199, line 21
  • The substitute measures the 1-year period from submission of the latest covered application, where the September text in section 1402(c) measured from the date it was determined complete. Nov. 7 p. 199, line 23
  • The substitute starts the 1-year period after a rehearing is complete, or after the deadline to seek rehearing expires, where Federal law provides for rehearing. Nov. 7 p. 200, line 5
  • The substitute adds the District of Columbia district court as an alternative venue for a complaint over a missed deadline. Nov. 7 p. 200, line 21

Division A, Title V: Nuclear Energy Licensing

Sec. 1501. Updating Hearing Procedures · New

  • The Nuclear Regulatory Commission may issue construction permits, operating licenses, combined licenses and amendments without a hearing when no one whose interest may be affected requests one, after 30 days notice in the Federal Register. Nov. 7 p. 203, line 15
  • Any hearing the Commission does hold under this subparagraph must use informal adjudicatory procedures. Nov. 7 p. 204, line 5
  • The section applies to all applications and proceedings pending before the Commission on or after enactment. Nov. 7 p. 204, line 24

Sec. 1502. Licensing of uranium enrichment facilities · New

  • Construction of a uranium enrichment facility may begin before its license is issued, on the same terms that apply to other fuel cycle facilities. Nov. 7 p. 205, line 10
  • An applicant must certify that it will notify the Governor, local governments and certain Tribes in writing at least 15 days before starting construction. Nov. 7 p. 206, line 7
  • The Commission keeps its authority to deny a license for failure to meet regulatory requirements, and the section does not affect NEPA, hearing rights or inspections. Nov. 7 p. 205, line 20
  • The Commission must issue or revise regulations within 180 days after enactment. Nov. 7 p. 207, line 1

Sec. 1503. Applicability of deadlines to certain nuclear facilities · New

  • Certain NEPA environmental assessment and impact statement deadlines and the permitting deadline in section 1403(b) of this Act do not apply to authorizations for nuclear reactors and other facilities licensed under the Atomic Energy Act. Nov. 7 p. 208, line 18
  • The exemption also covers authorizations issued under Atomic Energy Act sections 53, 63 or 81 and under parts 60 or 61 of title 10 of the Code of Federal Regulations. Nov. 7 p. 209, line 1

Sec. 1504. Compensation authority for career appointees · New

  • The Chairman of the Nuclear Regulatory Commission may set pay for a career appointee at up to 110 percent of the maximum basic pay for Senior Executive Service positions at the Commission. Nov. 7 p. 209, line 23
  • The authority applies regardless of when the career appointee was appointed and may not affect certification of the Commission's performance appraisal system. Nov. 7 p. 210, line 5
  • The annual report on compensation authority must also state the number of career appointees whose pay is fixed under the new subsection. Nov. 7 p. 211, line 9

Division A, Title Vi: Other Environmental Matters

Sec. 1601. Wildfire emissions prevention · New

  • A prescribed fire counts as an exceptional event for Clean Air Act air quality monitoring data, and the term includes cultural burning as determined by an Indian tribe. Nov. 7 p. 214, line 23
  • A state petition for an exceptional event designation is deemed approved if the EPA Administrator does not act within 1 year, unless the Administrator later determines the event is not an exceptional event. Nov. 7 p. 218, line 12
  • The Administrator must publish proposed revisions to the exceptional-event regulations within 270 days of enactment and finalize them within 180 days after that. Nov. 7 p. 215, line 4
  • The Administrator may make competitive grants, with a federal share of up to 90 percent, for assessing and controlling wildfire smoke hazards in community buildings including schools, with no more than 25 percent of each year's funds going to recipients in one State. Nov. 7 p. 220, line 23

Sec. 1602. Brownfields revitalization · New

  • Appropriations of $200,000,000 for each of fiscal years 2027 through 2031 are authorized for the brownfields grant and loan program. Nov. 7 p. 227, line 9
  • The remediation grant cap rises to $1,000,000 per site and the multipurpose grant cap rises from $1,000,000 to $2,000,000. Nov. 7 p. 221, line 14
  • Grant recipients may use up to 10 percent of the funds for demolition, subject to the EPA Administrator's approval. Nov. 7 p. 222, line 12
  • State and Tribal response program funding is authorized at $60 million in fiscal year 2027, rising by $5 million each year to $80 million in fiscal year 2031. Nov. 7 p. 228, line 7
  • Nonprofits described in section 501(c)(6) of the tax code become eligible entities for brownfields grants. Nov. 7 p. 221, line 7
  • The Administrator must waive the matching share requirement for entities in small communities or disadvantaged areas. Nov. 7 p. 226, line 23

Division B, Title I: Electric Transmission

Sec. 2101. Transmission permitting · Edited

  • The substitute drops the September provision that let FERC receive a permit application for facilities of 345 kilovolts or more at the same time as, or any time after, the state siting application. Sept. 30 p. 191, line 17
  • A transmitting utility, or an entity that will become one, must file a cost allocation tariff when FERC finds the permit criteria met, and the filing does not require a permit to have been issued. Nov. 7 p. 243, line 7
  • Facilities below 230 kilovolts also qualify for a permit if they are necessary to safely and reliably interconnect or operate a qualifying facility for which the permit is provided. Nov. 7 p. 239, line 21
  • FERC must also find that the project will facilitate just and reasonable rates, in addition to protecting consumers and providing improved reliability. Nov. 7 p. 239, line 14
  • The September wording that the facilities carry energy in interstate commerce, including from the outer Continental Shelf to a State, is replaced with interstate or foreign commerce. Sept. 30 p. 189, line 9
  • Tribal governments are listed with State and local governments as affected landowners. Nov. 7 p. 235, line 18
  • The ratepayer protection rule now covers customers of existing or proposed facilities whose costs are allocated under the cost allocation paragraph, and utilities may recover costs through voluntary agreement and allocate the remaining costs under the subsection. Nov. 7 p. 245, line 1

Sec. 2102. Reconductoring and other activities in existing rights-of-way · Edited

  • The additional area next to an existing right-of-way that counts for covered activities may extend no more than one-quarter mile from the right-of-way boundary. Nov. 7 p. 251, line 22
  • Covered transmission capacity activities and agency actions authorizing them are not considered a major Federal action under NEPA, where the September text designated them a category excluded from preparing an environmental assessment or impact statement. Nov. 7 p. 252, line 14
  • Installation of grid-enhancing advanced transmission technologies is added to the list of covered activities. Nov. 7 p. 253, line 10

Sec. 2103. Transmission planning · Edited

  • The definition of exempt transmitting utility covers a State or political subdivision, a Rural Electrification Act financed cooperative, or a cooperative selling under 4,000,000 megawatt hours a year, plus agencies and wholly owned corporations of those entities; the September text listed only a State or subdivision and a cooperative under that sales level. Nov. 7 p. 258, line 23
  • Rights, roles and opportunities given to States or their subdivisions in transmission planning must be given on a commensurate basis to Indian Tribes or their subdivisions. Nov. 7 p. 265, line 21
  • Planning must be conducted at least every 4 years, with the first planning cycle starting within 1 year after FERC approves the planning process, where the September text only required updates every 4 years. Nov. 7 p. 266, line 10
  • A FERC order or rulemaking on transmission planning in effect on the date of enactment counts as compliance with the section and relieves FERC of the requirements in subsection (c)(1). Nov. 7 p. 269, line 1
  • The cost allocation tariff filing duty applies to utilities that own, control, operate or propose to construct facilities selected in a regional or joint interregional plan, where the September text covered facilities constructed or modified as a result of the section. Nov. 7 p. 267, line 10
  • New text states that, except as expressly provided, nothing in the section confers, limits or impairs FERC authority under any other law. Nov. 7 p. 270, line 9

Sec. 2104. Improvements relating to local transmission planning and development · Edited

  • A referral to FERC for investigation of local transmission planning must now come from a State commission with jurisdiction over the utility, where the September text let States make the referral. Nov. 7 p. 272, line 7
  • The investigative team must issue a preliminary findings report within 120 days of the notice opening an investigation, and then a final report and recommendation to FERC. Nov. 7 p. 274, line 10
  • FERC must notice the final report for comment within 14 business days and issue an order within 120 days after that notice. Nov. 7 p. 275, line 7
  • The investigative team includes non-decisional individuals from an office designated by the FERC Chairman, where the September text named individuals from the Office of Enforcement and Regulatory Accounting. Nov. 7 p. 273, line 20
  • The fine of up to $5,000,000 per facility becomes a civil penalty credited to the transmission customers who bore the facility's costs, where the September text made the fine payable to the referring State. Nov. 7 p. 276, line 5
  • A local transmission facility qualifies only if it is located solely within a public utility transmission provider's retail distribution service territory and is not selected in a regional plan for cost allocation; the section also now covers replacement of facilities. Nov. 7 p. 271, line 4
  • FERC orders under the section are subject to rehearing and judicial review under section 313, and civil penalty orders follow section 31(d). Nov. 7 p. 277, line 5

Sec. 2105. Prohibition of right of first refusal · Edited

  • The ban on a federal right of first refusal for transmission facilities selected in a regional or interregional plan for cost allocation yields to any FERC rule or order in effect on the date of enactment, where the September text applied notwithstanding any such rule or order. Nov. 7 p. 277, line 16

Sec. 2106. Consolidated planning process · Edited

  • The consolidated planning rule applies to each Transmission Organization, where the September text applied to each transmission planning region. Nov. 7 p. 278, line 7
  • The consolidated process must publish planned interconnection locations for generating and storage capacity and for transmission capacity for customers, and an upfront, fixed, subregional per-megawatt fee, where the September text called for a zonal cost. Nov. 7 p. 278, line 22
  • The study process covers generator interconnection and transmission service requests, with withdrawal penalties applying after total interconnection costs and contingency limitations are given to the customer, and the process aims to co-optimize solutions to demand growth. Nov. 7 p. 279, line 12
  • FERC may find that current interconnection and planning processes comply with its rule, or that a Transmission Organization meets the rule's objectives through comparable processes. Nov. 7 p. 280, line 22
  • FERC approves the process if it meets the just and reasonable requirement of sections 205 and 206 and is consistent with the rule, where the September text asked whether it is consistent with the public interest. Nov. 7 p. 280, line 14

Sec. 2107. Ratepayer protection · Edited

  • The substitute drops the September definition of covered load, which set a floor of 20 megawatts; it uses computational load sites with no megawatt floor and leaves the definition of an eligible customer to FERC by rule or order. Sept. 30 p. 232, line 9
  • FERC must revise its 1994 transmission pricing policy statement and issue a notice of proposed rulemaking within 90 days of enactment, where the September text allowed 270 days for the policy statement and a further 270 days after its publication for the proposed rule. Nov. 7 p. 285, line 1
  • Eligible computational load customers pay the embedded cost and a reasonable assessment, as determined by FERC, of the incremental cost of transmission service, where the September text referred to the incremental cost itself. Nov. 7 p. 285, line 11
  • FERC may treat these customers as a customer class and assign some or all costs to the class rather than to individual customers. Nov. 7 p. 287, line 23
  • States may revise an existing arrangement to serve a computational load site, to the extent state law permits, to match the new rate rules from the effective date of the revision. Nov. 7 p. 298, line 4
  • The findings add a statement that computational load owners and operators bear the full cost of the electric service and infrastructure built to serve them, whether or not they take the service requested. Nov. 7 p. 281, line 8

Sec. 2108. Dispatchable energy systems market access · Edited

  • A qualifying behind-the-meter resource must have a rated capacity of at least 5 kilowatts and no more than 5 megawatts, where the September text set no upper limit. Nov. 7 p. 299, line 8
  • The list of practices that are unjust and unreasonable takes effect 18 months after enactment, and a Transmission Organization may set narrowly tailored performance rules for aggregations that join capacity markets but not energy markets. Nov. 7 p. 302, line 23
  • Metering, telemetry or equipment requirements are barred when more stringent than the statutory definitions, where the September text barred those inconsistent with the definitions. Nov. 7 p. 302, line 7
  • The bar on requiring energy market participation as a precondition to capacity market participation now applies when the energy participation is with compensation, and the capacity participation is on equal terms. Nov. 7 p. 302, line 12
  • The September provision barring distribution utilities from purposefully using control of the distribution system to keep eligible resources out of wholesale markets is removed, and the voluntary-compliance exception covers only subsections (b) and (c). Sept. 30 p. 253, line 18
  • The penalty for double compensation is three times the amount received, where the September text counted each day the compensation was knowingly retained. Sept. 30 p. 250, line 18

Sec. 2109. Grid-enhancing advanced transmission technologies · Edited (September 30: Sec. 2109. Advanced transmission technologies)

  • The section covers grid-enhancing advanced transmission technologies, defined as dynamic line rating, advanced power flow control, transmission topology optimization and high-performance transmission conductors, where the September text covered advanced transmission technology as defined in the Energy Policy Act of 2005. Nov. 7 p. 319, line 16
  • Public utilities must evaluate their systems for opportunities to deploy these technologies and use them on covered projects whenever the benefits would exceed the costs, where the September text required their use on covered projects without a benefit-cost condition. Nov. 7 p. 320, line 11
  • The best-available standard applies to a class of high-performance conductors that FERC determines is an economically reasonable alternative, taking into account reliability, safety and affordability. Nov. 7 p. 319, line 3
  • FERC also presumes that the costs of using a grid-enhancing technology are an economically reasonable alternative to other technologies. Nov. 7 p. 321, line 2
  • Deployment follows general schedules set by FERC, where the September text had FERC set a timeline for the deployment. Nov. 7 p. 322, line 17
  • The September amendment adding the four technologies to the Energy Policy Act of 2005 definition of advanced transmission technology is removed. Sept. 30 p. 264, line 22

Sec. 2110. Expediting generator interconnection queues · Edited

  • FERC must initiate and finalize a rulemaking at least once every 4 years on whether utilities are adequately deploying advanced computing technologies, where the September text required it to reopen the earlier rulemaking. Nov. 7 p. 324, line 10

Sec. 2111. Grid data access · Edited

  • A transmission provider's failure to comply with the grid data duties is subject to civil penalties under section 316A, where the September text treated it as a matter affecting the reliable operation of the bulk-power system under section 215. Nov. 7 p. 329, line 4
  • Covered studies include studies or analyses under sections 224, 225, 226, 228, 229 or 232 of the Federal Power Act, in addition to interconnection and similar tariff studies. Nov. 7 p. 325, line 7
  • For the study-related duties in subsection (b), an entity legally responsible for a covered study counts as a transmission provider. Nov. 7 p. 326, line 8
  • Each transmission provider must obtain, assemble and maintain grid data, where the September text added the duty to use it. Nov. 7 p. 326, line 17

Sec. 2112. Power marketing administrations · Edited

  • The section does not apply to any power marketing administration employee whose position, before March 27, 2025, was governed by a collective bargaining agreement covered by the two named federal pay laws. Nov. 7 p. 337, line 7
  • Chapter 71 of title 5 applies to all power marketing administration employees as it did on March 26, 2025, and collective bargaining agreements in effect on that date stay in force through their stated terms. Nov. 7 p. 337, line 21
  • Pay must be comparable to the electric industry in the locality of the administration, where the September text referred to the public sectors of the electric industry and to consumer-owned utilities. Nov. 7 p. 332, line 22
  • The pay authority applies except as otherwise provided in the section, where the September text applied notwithstanding any other law, rule, regulation or directive on Federal employee pay other than chapter 83 of title 5. Nov. 7 p. 331, line 15

Sec. 2113. FERC fee assessments · Edited

  • The section is renumbered as section 318 of the Federal Power Act and defines the Commission by a short reference instead of repeating its full name. Nov. 7 p. 338, line 3

Sec. 2114. Collection of data on computational load entities · Edited

  • The data collection term changes from “data center entity,” an end-user or group of co-located end-users whose primary purpose is housing computing equipment, to “computational load site,” one or more buildings or installations at a single location that primarily contain information technology infrastructure. Nov. 7 p. 338, line 22
  • The listed uses are software services, artificial intelligence, cryptocurrency mining and other computational activities at a data center, where the September text listed AI, cloud computing or data hosting, and cryptocurrency or other digital-asset computation. Nov. 7 p. 339, line 5
  • The Energy Information Administration must revise Form 861 or any successor form to separately include information from computational load sites, where the September text required it to include load from data center entities. Nov. 7 p. 339, line 18

Sec. 2115. De novo review of civil penalties under the Natural Gas Act · New

  • Civil penalties assessed under section 22 of the Natural Gas Act are reviewed under the same procedures that apply to civil penalties under section 31(d) of the Federal Power Act. Nov. 7 p. 339, line 23

Sec. 2116. Transmission Organization governance · New

  • The text states that FERC has jurisdiction over the corporate governance of a Transmission Organization for purposes of Federal Power Act section 205. Nov. 7 p. 340, line 7
  • The same statement is added to section 206, covering FERC review of existing rates and practices. Nov. 7 p. 340, line 13

Sec. 2117. Data Center Community Assistance Program · New
