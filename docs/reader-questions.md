# Reader questions

What people ask and argue about the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (S. 5653), and how many taps and how much scrolling each answer takes on this site at 375×812. Collected on October 21, 2026.

## How the questions were collected

A Haiku research agent ran web searches and fetched pages from news outlets, advocacy groups, law firms, trade groups and think tanks, and logged 361 questions. 354 of them name a source URL, from 110 pages, and are linked below with the link text showing the page's domain. Only the 203 from the 41 pages the agent actually fetched are counted.

The other 151 come from search-result summaries: the agent never opened the linked page, so a summary may have mixed in another result. They are marked "summary" below, shown in their own column in the topic table, and left out of every other count until someone opens the page and confirms it. The last 7 name an outlet but no page, so they are listed at the end as leads and left out of every count.

The agent often logged several questions from one page, so the topic table gives both: pages are distinct source URLs, questions are logged entries. Neither says how many people asked.

A second pass read X threads in Chrome (search "BAAJA since:2026-09-15", top posts, and "permitting reform" senate, latest). Reddit was blocked in the browser tool and Bluesky search needs a login, so neither is covered. Heatmap is paywalled; only its search summaries are logged. Pages that failed: Akin Gump (403), NRDC's BAAJA assessment (403), Energy-Storage.news (403), the Senate ENR site (maintenance redirect), and the Sierra Club (empty page). Some Earthjustice and E&E entries concern EPRA 2024 or the SPEED Act rather than S. 5653; they are kept because readers carry those questions over.

## What this changed on the site

The site already answered most questions in one tap, so the change cuts controls instead of adding a questions page (DESIGN.md, 2026-10-21):

1. **Fewer controls on the same pages (chosen).** One filter row per page, the rest behind a More filters control; no jump row on pages under three screens; the status fold above the provisions. Every route and every record stays where it was. Media drops from 38 controls on its first screen to 4.
2. **Merge the News pages.** Timeline and Media as one feed, People inside Positions: nine pages become seven. Fewer subtabs, but the feeds have different shapes and old links would need redirects.
3. **A questions-first home page.** The Overview opens with the eight most-asked questions, each linking to its answer. It matches the research most directly, but the provisions list already answers most of them in one tap, so it would repeat them.
